# Three Marks modpack

Game-client modpack for «Мир танков» (Lesta, RU realm): Python 2.7 scripts the client loads from `mods/<client version>/`. It ships as `.mtmod` packages (Lesta 1.35+; `.wotmod` for WG clients): the core runtime, the companion, and one package per feature. The pre-split single package is still available as a build option.

It does the following, plus one opt-in:

- after each battle, it sends the player's own battle results to Three Marks in signed batches;
- it records marks-of-excellence (MoE) percentages for the player's own vehicles;
- in battle, it shows a movable MoE panel: the percent at the start and projected after the battle, the damage to the next goal and, in its extended style, the damage to 65/85/95% and for +0.5% (see [marks_panel](#marks_panel--moe-panel-in-battle));
- in the hangar, a component of its own shows the Tank card of the selected tank: the marks on the gun, the percent with its last change and the trend of the last battles, the bar of the mark steps with the average each needs, the damage per battle to the next mark and a battles forecast at the player's pace, and the marks history in the window;
- in the hangar, it shows the Session card with battles, win rate, average damage and WN8, and once bound the goals the player set on the site and the account line read over signed `/mod/me` requests;
- in battle, optional HUD components on a shared draggable panel layer: the battle log (own shots, assist and the hits on the player), the battle progress plate (main gun, tank record, battle WN8), team HP, sixth-sense alert, the own equipment, the own gun's traverse limits and platoon points; plus a battle chat filter, a streamer mode and the 15 m circle (see [Battle HUD](#battle-hud) and [Battle extras](#battle-extras)). No component repeats another or the stock client ([spec](../../../docs/specs/2026-09-30-hud-consolidation-and-design.md) §0);
- in the hangar, an extended battle summary with the session's battles, a marks-of-excellence history per vehicle, a clock/server/ping panel, auto-resupply flags, a notification filter, a cleaner hangar, quick actions and client-settings presets (see [Hangar and client-settings components](#hangar-and-client-settings-components));
- opt-in, off by default: it uploads the replays the game itself recorded of the player's own battles (see [Replay auto-upload](#replay-auto-upload)).

## Fair play

The mod follows the Lesta Fair Play Policy (see [market research](../../../docs/research/competitors/market.md#техника-модов)) and the `Fair play` rule in [CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md).

- **It reads only the player's own data:**
  - the `personal` block of the player's own battle results;
  - the player's own vehicle dossier;
  - the player's own damage and assist feedback events, which also drive the vanilla damage log and ribbons;
  - the player's own queue events;
  - the loadout of the player's own selected vehicle in the hangar (equipment, consumables, directives, loaded shells, field modifications, crew skills), read when the player joins the queue;
  - an account command about the player's own vehicle.
- **It never reads or shows enemy information.** It has no positions, reload timers, aim or gun-marker data, spotting beyond vanilla, or minimap markers.
- **One read of other players, not combat information:** `pack_badge` sends the account ids of the battle's players (the arena data behind the stock player panels, without anonymised players) to the site once per battle and marks the ones who use the bound mod and chose to show it ([pack_badge](#pack_badge--the-three-marks-badge-by-names)).
- **It has no aim assist.** It never changes vehicle parameters or computes anything about aiming. The crosshair, camera and minimap components only switch options the game's own settings window already offers (reticle look, sniper zoom steps, stabilisation, minimap size and own range circles), in the hangar, when the player picks them; the smaller aim circle (`aim_circle`) only draws the game's own circle smaller (see [Hangar and client-settings components](#hangar-and-client-settings-components)).
- **Hangar-only components stay in the hangar:** the replay manager touches only the player's own replay files (its opt-in auto names too); the quick actions are the client's own free requests (removable equipment, crew to barracks, the previous crew back), each confirmed by the player; auto-resupply sends the client's own vehicle-settings requests only when the player presses its button; the notification filter and the cleaner hangar only hide promo elements the client would show.
- **Battle extras only touch the player's own view:** the chat filter only hides or timestamps chat lines the client already received and never filters the player's own lines.
- **It never serialises other players' data from battle results.** The `vehicles`, `players` and `avatars` blocks are never read into the payload. The test `test_payload.BattleEventTest.test_never_leaks_other_players` enforces this.
- The only other-vehicle value it reads is the team of a vehicle the player damaged. It uses that to ignore team damage in the MoE panel. The client already shows this value.
- **The battle HUD components only re-arrange what the client already shows the player** (see [Battle HUD](#battle-hud)): the player's own damage/assist/blocked/received feedback, the hit result the client draws on an enemy marker after the player's own shot, the vehicle HP the client already has for markers and team panels, the arena timer, the client's own sixth-sense lamp. Nothing from Lesta's forbidden list: no enemy reload timers, aim direction, arty trajectories or tracer positions, destroyed-object or lost-enemy markers, smart crosshair or in-battle armour analysis.
- **Grey components are kept off by default:** auto_reserves (a hangar request without a button press), hangar_tweaks' exact interface scale and 3–5 carousel rows (beyond the game's own options), and replay_upload (a replay file holds every participant's data). The per-component verdicts against Lesta's list are in the [fair-play audit](../../../docs/research/data/2026-10-05-fair-play-audit.md).
- **Nothing is collected or sent until the player binds the mod.** Binding uses a one-time code from the site. Each feature can be switched off. The exceptions, decided per call: three unsigned `GET`s of public reference data that carry no account, device or battle data, only the mod version in the `User-Agent` — `update_notice` (the game version folder, for the latest release), `preset_advisor` (a tank id, for the site's public build advice) and `core/client/moe` (a tank id, for the tank's public MoE thresholds; it adds the device id only once bound). They read what anyone can open on the site; the owner can gate them behind binding instead. The last one, the owner's decision: the `pack_badge` plate's presence, so every install is marked as Near_You marks its users — one unsigned `POST /mod/badges/presence` per own battle (never in a replay, one more for the players of a vehicle added later), only while `pack_badge` and the whole mod are on, carrying the own account id, the battle's account ids (without bots and anonymised players) and the «show my badge» switch; the server keeps only a 30-day presence key per account, deleted when the switch is off.
- **Requests go only to the production API over verified TLS** (`core/net/transport`): the certificate chain and host name are checked, redirects are refused (they would carry the signed headers elsewhere), answers are capped in size; `server_url` is honoured only in a development install (see the config table).
- **Replay auto-upload is opt-in** (`upload_replays`, off by default). It never turns replay recording on and never touches the battle: in the hangar it uploads the `.wotreplay` file the client already wrote, only when the replay header's `playerID` is the bound account. The file is the player's own recording, exactly as the site's manual upload accepts it. Uploads stay private unless `publish_replays` (also off by default) is on; the server re-checks the recorder and refuses anyone else's replay.

Every package keeps its tests in its own `tests/` folder. The build leaves `tests/` out and moves `entry/` scripts to `gui/mods/`.

### Load order

The client imports every `gui/mods/mod_*.pyc` in hash order, so packages have no load order.

- The core has no entry script. It starts on first use.
- The companion's `mod_otmetki` creates the app. At the end of `start()` the app binds itself to the core registry: `core.registry.registry().bind(app)`.
- Each feature's `mod_otmetki_<id>` calls `register()`. A feature registered before the app starts is attached when the app binds; one registered later is attached at once. Both calls are idempotent.

`packages/core/tests/test_core_runtime.py` runs every order. `tools/tests/test_client_smoke.py` imports the real entry scripts in shuffled orders against stubbed client modules, then plays a login and a battle through the hooks.

### The app and its events

`companion/app/client` is a thin host. It hooks the client events and hands them to the companion's capture modules (`battles/client`, `marks/client`, `binding/client`) and to the features through `app.bus`.

- **Host interface for features:** `config`, `translate` (features add their strings with `translate.catalog.add(STRINGS)`), `ui`, `transport`, `account_id`, `in_battle`, `marks.hangar_moe`, `is_bound()`, `current_credentials()`, `auth_failed`, `on_auth_failed()`, `user_agent()`, `config_dir`, and the state file: `state`, `register_state(key, dump)`, `save_state()`.
- **Bus events:** `account`, `rebind`, `hangar`, `vehicle_moe`, `enqueued`, `dequeued`, `battle_enter`, `battle_start`, `battle_ready`, `battle_leave`, `battle_results`, `battle_event`, `battle_recorded`, `ingest_response`, `tick`. Their arguments are in the docstring of `companion/app/client`. Emitted by packages: `component_settings(component_id, changed_keys)` and `language(language)` (the in-game window), `hud_edit(active)` and `hud_describe(collect)` (HUD edit mode, see [In-game UI](#in-game-ui)), `replay_uploaded(arena_unique_id, replay_id)` (replay upload).
- **Settings window:** `companion/settings_ui/client`. `SettingsView` is the interface (`register()`, `refresh()`). The app starts with the null `SettingsView` (no window); the ui package replaces it with its Gameface window through `attach_settings_view(app, view)`, so the app keeps calling `app.settings_ui.refresh()`. There is no other settings stack: without OpenWG Gameface, `config.json` is edited by hand.

## Data flow

1. **Binding.** On the site, a signed-in user gets a 10-character code: alphabet `ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZ23456789`, one-time, short TTL. In the hangar, the user enters it in the settings window (the «Три отметки» entry of ModsList) and presses «Привязать». Without the window, the user sets `"bind_code"` in `mods/configs/otmetki/config.json` and logs in.
   - The mod sends `POST /mod/bind` `{code, account_id, mod_version, client_version, realm}`.
   - The server answers `{device_id, secret, account_id}`.
   - The credentials are stored per account in `credentials.json`: the game-folder copy holds only `{device_id, account_id, bound_at}`, the `%APPDATA%\TriOtmetki` copy adds the secret sealed with DPAPI (see [Durable settings](#durable-settings-appdatatriotmetki)).
2. **Capture.** Events go into a per-account persistent outbox (`outbox_<account_id>.json`, at most 2000 events):
   - hangar MoE snapshots, sent only when the values change;
   - MoE distribution, once per vehicle per day;
   - queue times;
   - own battle results. These are deduplicated by `arenaUniqueID`, and the last 200 are kept in `state.json`.
3. **Send.** In the hangar only, every 15 s (`flush_interval_seconds`, a fixed value) and right after a battle result, the mod sends one batch of up to 50 events with `POST /mod/ingest`. Headers:
   - `X-Otmetki-Device: <device_id>`
   - `X-Otmetki-Timestamp: <unix seconds>` and `X-Otmetki-Nonce: <random hex, new per request>`
   - `X-Otmetki-Signature: sha256=<hex HMAC-SHA256(secret, "v2\nPOST\n<path>\n<timestamp>\n<nonce>\n" + raw body)>`. The server refuses a timestamp more than 5 minutes off (428, retried) and a nonce it has already seen, so a captured request cannot be replayed or pointed at another path.
   - **Clock skew.** Every error reply on `/mod/*` carries `X-Otmetki-Server-Time: <unix seconds>` (plus the standard `Date`). On a 428 the mod stores `server time − local time` as its clock offset, re-signs the same body with a fresh nonce and retries once right away; every later request (ingest, settings poll, apply result) is signed with the corrected clock. A second 428, or a 428 without a usable time, goes to the normal backoff. The offset lives in memory and is recomputed after a restart.

   Only one batch is in flight at a time. Events leave the outbox only after a 2xx or 409 response, so a crash or restart never loses them. Retries use exponential backoff (5 s up to 10 min, ±20% jitter) and honour `Retry-After`. Other responses:

   | Response        | Mod behaviour                                   |
   | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
   | 401 / 403       | Pauses the outbox and asks the player to rebind |
   | 400 / 404 / 422 | Drops the batch                                 |
   | 413             | Halves the batch size                           |

4. **Session.** The session aggregates locally. It counts random battles only (`bonus_type == 1`) and starts a new session after `session_idle_minutes` (60 by default) without activity. The ingest response may carry `session: {session_id, wn8}`, and the hangar panel then shows the server-computed WN8.
5. **MoE panel.** When a vehicle is selected in the hangar, the mod fetches `GET /v1/moe/{tank_id}` (`core/client/moe`, shared by the panel and the hangar view) and caches it for 6 h (a failed read is retried after 10 min and keeps the older curve). In battle, the panel shows (see [marks_panel](#marks_panel--moe-panel-in-battle)):
   - the percent at the start of the battle from the dossier, and the projection: the dossier percent plus the curve's change between the EMA before and after this battle;
   - the combined damage still needed this battle for 65/85/95% (and 100% when it is next), and for +0.1/0.5/1%;
   - the battles to the next mark at the pace of the player's own last battles on the tank.

- **Envelope:** `schema_version=1`, `batch_id`, `device_id`, `account_id`, `realm="RU"`, `mod_version`, `client_version`, `sent_at`, `events[1..50]`.
- **`battle_result` fields:**
  - identity:
    - `event_id="battle:<arenaUniqueID>"`, which makes the event idempotent;
    - `arena_unique_id`, a string because 64-bit values do not fit in a JS number;
    - `arena_type_id`, `map_name`, `bonus_type`, `gui_type`;
  - outcome: `duration_s`, `result` (win / loss / draw), `team`, `winner_team`;
  - `vehicle{tank_id, name, tier}`, where `tank_id` is the same as the Lesta API `tank_id`;
  - `stats{…}`: damage, radio/track/stun assist, blocked, spotted, frags, shots, direct and penetrating enemy hits, xp, credits, life time, alive, premium;
  - `moe{marks_on_gun, damage_rating (%×100), moving_avg_damage}` after the battle. This is the raw data point for our own RU thresholds;
  - `queue_time_s`, `session_id`;
  - `loadout` (optional, null when unknown): the own vehicle's `optional_devices`, `consumables`, `directives` (intCD per slot, null for empty), `shells[{shell_id, count}]`, `field_modifications[]`, `crew[{role, skills[] in learning order}]`, `gameplay_id` (`arena_type_id >> 16`). It feeds the site's recommended builds.
  - `platoon` (optional, null when solo): `{size}` — the size of the own platoon after the battle, counted from the players with the own `prebattleID` on the own team. No other player's account id or name leaves the client (`test_payload.test_platoon_counts_own_team_mates_and_sends_no_ids`).
  - `shots` (optional, null when off or none): own shots that damaged an enemy, from the player feedback events — `damage`, `nominal` (armour damage of the own shell), `shell`, `outcome`, `distance_m` (null: the mod reads no enemy positions), `fatal`. Feeds «Честный рандом».
- **`moe_snapshot`:** `tank_id`, `damage_rating`, `moving_avg_damage`, `marks_on_gun`, `battles`.
- **`queue`:** `queue_type`, `wait_s`, `outcome` (arena / dequeued), `tank_id`.
- **`battle_start`:** `tank_id` of the own vehicle when the arena is created, flushed at once. Opens the streamer's Twitch auto-prediction; the matching `battle_result` resolves it.
- **Server obligations:**
  - verify the HMAC over the raw bytes it received, never over a re-serialisation;
  - check that the device belongs to `account_id`;
  - deduplicate by `event_id`;
  - on bind, reject an `account_id` that differs from the Lesta ID linked to the site user.

## Streamer settings (hangar only)

Spec: [streamer-settings §3.5](../../../docs/specs/2026-09-26-streamer-settings.md). Switch: `share_settings` (on by default; does nothing until the mod is bound). Client glue: `packages/companion/settings_share/client`.

- **Whitelist.** The glue reads standard client settings through the settings core (`dependency.instance(ISettingsCore)`) into flat keys (`fov`, `sniperSens`, `zoomSteps`, …, see `settings_share.FIELDS`). `build_export` keeps only those keys with valid values and nests them into the contract groups `display` … `battleUi`. Login/account keys, hardware and mods are never sent.
- **Export.** Set `"settings_action": "export"` in `config.json` and open the hangar. The mod posts `POST /mod/settings` `{device_id, account_id, mod_version, target, anonymous_stats, settings}`. `settings_target` is `private` (default) or `profile`; `settings_anonymous_stats` is off by default.
- **Apply.** Every 2 minutes in the hangar the mod polls `POST /mod/settings/apply/poll`. For a request it shows a yes/no dialog with the diff. Resolution, refresh rate, window mode and sensitivity are left out unless `settings_include_resolution` / `settings_include_sensitivity` are on. On confirm it applies the diff and posts `…/apply/<id>/result` `{status: applied}`; on cancel `rejected`. Without a dialog API the request stays pending; nothing is applied automatically. No copy of the previous values is kept.
- It never installs or configures third-party mods. All three requests are signed like `/mod/ingest`.

## Session report sharing (opt-in)

Switches: `share_session_report` (off by default; does nothing until the mod is bound) and `share_session_channel` (`telegram` (default), `discord`, `both`), in the session panel's card. Profiles and codes never carry `share_session_report`. Contract: [contract/session-share.schema.json](contract/session-share.schema.json). A 409 `channel_not_linked` on the switch shows one notice («привяжите выбранный канал на сайте») and the mod stops asking until the switch or the channel changes (or the next game session); other failures retry after 5 minutes.

- **Server flag:** when the player turns it on (or changes the channel), the hangar posts `POST /mod/me/session-share` `{device_id, account_id, enabled, channels}`; while the flag is on, the server posts the session card to the player's own Telegram / Discord linked on the site when the live session ends. Turning it off posts `enabled: false`. ## Replay auto-upload

Switches: `upload_replays` (off by default; does nothing until the mod is bound) and `publish_replays` (off by default: uploads are private). The replay manager's «Загрузить на сайт» asks for one replay through `core.events.EVENT_REPLAY_UPLOAD_REQUEST`: under the same switch and binding it is queued to go next, even one an earlier try gave up on. Without a start time seen in battle, the results' `arenaCreateTime` goes through the client's `helpers.time_utils.makeLocalServerTime`. Feature `features/replay_upload`: pure logic in `model/` (`files` lookup and multipart, `queue`, `upload`, `timing`, `constants`; the header reader is `core/replay_file`); client glue in `client/`. Contract: [contract/replay-upload.schema.json](contract/replay-upload.schema.json).

1. **Respecting the game setting.** The mod never enables recording. If the client's replay setting (`replayEnabled` in the settings core) reads as off, nothing is queued; if it cannot be read, the mod just looks for a file and gives up when none appears.
2. **Queue.** When the player's own battle results arrive (the same hook as `battle_result`, never during replay playback), the battle is queued in `replays_<account_id>.json`: `arenaUniqueID`, account, local start time (from `onAvatarReady`, else `arenaCreateTime` corrected by the clock offset). The queue deduplicates by `arenaUniqueID` against pending items and the last 500 finished ones, holds at most 50 battles and forgets a battle after 7 days. It survives a client restart.
3. **Finding the file.** From 30 s after the battle, in the hangar only, the mod scans the client replay folder (`BattleReplay`'s replay dir, else `replays/`) for `.wotreplay`/`.mtreplay` files, skipping `temp.wotreplay` and anything older than the battle. It reads only the JSON header blocks: the file matches when `playerID` is the bound account and the results block names the same `arenaUniqueID`; a replay without a results block (left before the end) matches by its `dateTime` within 5 minutes of the battle start. A file modified in the last 5 s is still being written and is retried in 15 s. With the "last battle only" setting the file is overwritten by the next battle, and the header check then refuses it. No match within 30 minutes drops the battle.
4. **Upload.** One upload at a time, on a background thread (`BackgroundRunner` + blocking `SyncTransport`, 120 s timeout); the main thread only starts jobs and applies results. The file is refused above **50 MiB** (`max_bytes` in the contract, same as the server) before it is read. It is posted as `multipart/form-data` (one part named `file`) to `POST /replays/mod`, with the `/mod/ingest` headers, `X-Otmetki-Visibility` and `signing.signed_request`; the HMAC covers the raw file bytes (`"v2\nPOST\n/replays/mod\n<timestamp>\n<nonce>\nx-otmetki-visibility:<visibility>\n" + file`), because that is what the server verifies. A 428 re-syncs the clock from `X-Otmetki-Server-Time` or `Date` and re-signs once. The server has no chunked or resumable upload, so the file goes in one request. Entering a battle pauses the uploader: nothing new starts, and a running upload stops at its next 8 KiB block (`core.net.transport.StoppableBody`, sent with a Content-Length); it is sent again from the hangar without a backoff. A replay renamed between the search and the read is searched for again by its header once.
5. **Visibility.** Uploads are private (only the owner sees them on the site) unless `publish_replays` is on (off by default), which sends `X-Otmetki-Visibility: public`. The value is taken when the upload starts. The header is a signed header: its `name:value` line sits between the nonce and the body in the signed message, so it cannot be added, stripped or changed on the way. The server stores a request without the header as private and refuses `unlisted` or any other value (400).
6. **Ownership.** The server parses the replay header before storing it and answers 422 `replay_not_owned` when its recorder (`playerID`) is not the device's bound account. Every error reply on `/replays/mod` carries `X-Otmetki-Server-Time`, as on `/mod/*`.

| Response                                                               | Mod behaviour                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2xx / 409 (same file already uploaded)                                 | Done, remembered                                               |
| 401, 403 other than the plan limit                                     | Pauses replay uploads and asks the player to rebind            |
| 403 `SUBSCRIPTION_REQUIRED`                                            | Stored-replays limit: retried in 6 h                           |
| 400 / 404 / 413 / 422 (`replay_not_owned` included), or file too large | Dropped, remembered                                            |
| network error, 428 twice, 429, 5xx                                     | Exponential backoff 30 s up to 1 h, ±20% jitter, `Retry-After` |

## Battle HUD

Eighteen components (the MoE panel and seventeen others; the damage log's last-hit pop-up is a second panel of its package), each its own feature package (`features/<id>/`, own `.mtmod`, depends on core and companion) with a switch in `config.json` (on by default) and its own section in `mods/configs/otmetki/components.json`. They follow the research's "who does it best": XVM for template-formatted logs, Battle Observer for team HP and the clock (Battle Observer does not run on Lesta), everyone's sixth-sense lamp without the forbidden "nearest enemy".

### The HUD layer (core)

- **`core/hud/`** (pure): `HudLayer` (`register(panel_id, schema)`, `show(panel_id, text)`, `hide`, `hide_all`, `update_settings`, `on_moved`), `ComponentConfig` (components.json: one schema-checked section per component; sections of components that are not installed are kept), `panel_schema(...)` (the common layout keys plus the panel's own), `HudBackend` (the renderer interface), `render(template, values)` (`{macro}` templates on the standard library's `string.Template`; `{{` is a literal brace, an unknown macro stays as written).
- **Common panel keys** (every panel section): `x`, `y` (-4000..4000), `align_x` (`left`/`center`/`right`), `align_y` (`top`/`center`/`bottom`), `alpha` (0..100), `drag`, `scale` (50..300, Gameface only); `font_size` (14 unless the panel names its own) and `border` (off) are fixed (`PANEL_FIXED`, answered by `Settings.get`). The on/off switch stays in `config.json` (the companion schema), so the settings window keeps working while a component is not installed.
- **Dragging:** panels let the mouse through until they can be moved. In the hangar, where the cursor is always shown, that is while the player holds the edit modifier (config.json `hud_modifier`: `alt` by default, `ctrl_alt` when Alt clashes with the client, `ctrl`, `shift`; «Клавиша перемещения панелей в ангаре» in the window; `core/client/hud/modifier` watches the game's `InputHandler` key events and polls the release). In battle it is whenever the cursor is shown (Ctrl, the stock way): no second key. The cursor state comes from `GameEvent.SHOW_CURSOR`/`HIDE_CURSOR` and is read again from `GUI.mcursor().visible` a frame after every key event and when the HUD window opens (no event fires when another view already holds the cursor). Then the Gameface page frames every movable panel, lets the player drag one and resizes it with the wheel (`scale`, 50–300 %); on release the frames go and the position stays. The page reads the press and the wheel on its view and hit-tests the panels' rects itself (`widgets/hud-overlay/lib/hit-panel`), so an icon, a plate or an SVG of a widget under the pointer can never swallow the drag, and images are never dragged by the engine. The page sends `moved` with the nearest anchor or `resized`, and the layer writes `x`/`y` (and `align_x`/`align_y`, `scale`) into the panel's section, so it comes back there next battle. Hangar labels (`app.ui.show(alias, text, layout, on_moved)`, or `core.hud.HangarLabel(app, alias).show(text, layout, on_moved)`, which redraws only a changed text and takes the label off for None) save the same way into their component's section (`FeatureComponent.save_place`); a label without a place of its own (no `on_moved`) keeps the player's drag in `state.json` (`hangar_places`, `core.hud.label.hangar_places`), and `hud_reset_layout` clears it. «Сбросить расположение» in the HUD card (`hud_reset_all`) puts every panel, the hangar labels (`hud_reset_layout` on the bus) and the settings button back to their defaults.
- **Docked columns (`core/hud/panel` `DOCKS`, `DOCK_ANCHORS`):** panels that belong together share one anchor and stack instead of overlapping, whatever their height. Battle anchors are in design px, which are the stock Scaleform battle page's own px at every interface scale (its stage is the screen over the scale, and the Gameface root font size is the same scale), measured against the RU 1.45 AS3 layout: the team lists (up to 368 px wide from about y 45), the score strip, the timer, the damage panel and the chat above it, the consumables (58 px from the bottom, centred), the minimap and the sixth sense lamp. Under the score strip the page keeps the capture bars and the quest progress, so no column starts in the middle of the top edge. Battle `battle_left_bottom` (the damage log, in the stock log's place right of the damage panel) and `battle_left_top` (platoon points; right of the left team list, under the score strip); the clock sits left of the stock timer, and a team HP strip that keeps the stock score strip sits right of it in the same row; hangar `hangar_left` (marks, marks history, battle wounds, ratings; under the crew) and `hangar_right` (session, goals, personal missions, platoon; under the vehicle parameters). The default battle set (team HP, damage and hit logs with the last hit, marks, reload, the equipment row, the lamp) overlaps no stock area and no other panel on every screen the client allows for 1920×1080 (scale 1), 2560×1440 (1–1.5) and 3840×2160 (1–2; `gui/shared/utils/graphics.py` `_SCALES` offers no scale above 1 at 1080p), checked with the layout harness against the AS3 stock boxes; `tools/tests/test_battle_layout.py` checks every default battle place against the capture bars and the quest progress on each of those screens; the secondary panels are opt-in (`OPT_IN_FEATURES`). A panel at its group's anchor gets `dock: {group, order, reserve}`; the page (`widgets/hud-overlay/lib/dock`) puts the first at the anchor, each next one under the one before (above it for a bottom anchor), and starts a new column beside the first, towards the middle, when a panel would cross `reserve` (the strip kept free for the carousel, the chat or the minimap); a hangar column that does not fit first moves up, never above the header nor into a panel above it (battle columns carry a `ceiling` at their anchor and never move up). Dragging a panel takes it out of its column (its `dock` becomes None).
- **Attached places (`core/hud/panel` `ATTACHED`, docs/research/competitors/2026-10-05-behavior-parity.md):** four battle panels take the place the established packs give them, measured from the stock element beside them: the marks right of the stock consumables panel (left edge 12 px right of it, its bottom on the panel's bottom, flush with the screen's edge as the stock bottom elements; above the panel's right half, over the equipment row, when it would reach the minimap), the equipment row centred 6 px above the slot number labels the stock panel draws over its slots (they take 6 px over the panel's top; the client centres the panel on the screen: `ConsumablesPanel.updatePosition`, as kurzdor's battleequipment sits), «Прогресс боя» right of the team HP strip (left edge 308 px right of the middle, y 4; under the strip below 1700 design px), the previous battle's card above the minimap. While such a panel sits at its default place the layer sends `attach: {kind, bar, minimap}` with the consumables panel's width (57 px per slot it added, its `_mask`, re-measured after each slot change) and the minimap side (the `minimapSize` account setting: 210/260/310/390/490/610), read by `core/client/hud/stock` (`metrics.py`); fallbacks 7 slots and 310 px. The page (`views/hud/lib/attach`) places it from them; the default place itself is the fallback until the stock element is measured. A dragged panel keeps its own place. The sizes are read again whenever the battle page shows or hides the consumables panel and on every minimap resize in battle (`MinimapComponent.applyNewSize`). **Followed stock elements (`core/hud/cover` `FollowedComponents`, `HudLayer.set_stock_hidden`):** `core/client/hud/cover` reads from the page's own `_setComponentsVisibility` calls (and `as_isComponentVisibleS` when a page appears and on every check) whether the consumables panel and the minimap are off the screen; the pre-battle setups panel (`PrebattleAmmunitionPanelViewMeta.as_showS` / `as_hideS`) counts as hiding the consumables panel, as the battle page hides it in Flash then. A panel in `core/hud/panel` `FOLLOWS` goes with its element (the equipment row: gone after death, in the video camera and under the setups panel, back on a Frontline respawn, as kurzdor's battleequipment), and `attach` carries 0 for a hidden element: the marks, which stay after death as the gunmarks packs keep theirs, then sit right of the stock post-mortem tips (`postmortemTips_bg`, 501 x 72) or above them when the minimap leaves no room, and the previous battle's card drops to the bottom right corner. «Прогресс боя» follows the screen centre, never a measured size.
- **Hangar view (`core/lobby_view`, `core/client/lobby_view`):** hangar labels show only over the plain hangar. The rule is the stock hangar's own for its vehicle markers (RU 1.45 `LobbyVehicleMarkerView._canShowMarkers`): the hangar's `SFWindow` (`VIEW_ALIAS.LOBBY_HANGAR`) is alive and no other window of the `SUB_VIEW`, `TOP_SUB_VIEW`, `FULLSCREEN_WINDOW` or `OVERLAY` layers is (pop-overs aside), re-checked on every `IGuiLoader.windowsManager.onWindowStatusChanged`. The battle queue (`battleQueue`), the store, research, profile, barracks, the prebattle rooms, the settings window, rewards and full-screen Gameface views hide them; `app.ui` sends `visible: false` to every label (`Ui.set_context`) and brings them back unchanged, and `hangar_marks` (a HUD layer panel) hides itself the same way. Without the windows manager nothing is hidden.
- **Updates:** `show()` sends a text only when it changed; `place(panel_id, x, y)` moves a shown panel for now without touching components.json (the crosshair mark follows the reticle through it).
- **Renderer:** `core/client/hud/` builds one backend, **OpenWG Gameface** (`HUD renderer: gameface` in the log), or a `NullBackend` when it does not import (`HUD: OpenWG Gameface: No module named ..., battle and hangar panels are off`); the layer and the hangar labels (`app.ui`) share it.
  - **Gameface** (`core/client/hud/gameface`, docs/specs/2026-10-06-gameface-inject-host.md): available when `frameworks.wulf`, `gui.impl.pub` and `openwg_gameface` import and `res_id_by_key('otmetki/ui/hud')` resolves (the ui package ships the page; OpenWG Gameface validated its res_map). In the lobby `hud.html` is drawn inside the Scaleform hangar view (`core/client/hud/inject_page`, below); in battle the backend opens one transparent `WindowImpl` (`WindowFlags.WINDOW`, `WindowLayer.WINDOW`) with `hud.html` a frame after the battle GUI space was entered, keeps it while the battle lasts and closes it when the battle space is left. Nothing is injected into the battle page: 0.3.7 drew the battle panels there and the client crashed natively (no Python traceback) within minutes of two battles, so 0.3.8 went back to the window. `core/hud/surface.HudSurface` keeps the labels with the GUI space they were created in (a hangar label never shows in battle) and encodes `{v: 6, cursor, edit, hover, panels: [{id, text, x, y, align_x, align_y, alpha, drag, border, visible, scale, widget, dock, attach, hint}]}` into the page view model's `state`; the page of the current GUI space (the hangar page in the lobby, the window in battle) answers `ready`, `moved`, `resized`, `mouse`, `drawn` and `area` (a message of the hangar page is not heard in battle). The label changes of one frame (the 10 Hz reload timer next to the clock and the logs) become one push on the next frame (`core/hud/surface` `FramePush`, `BigWorld.callback(0)`), and a state equal to the last one sent is not pushed again; the page keeps the panel objects that did not change (`widgets/hud-overlay/lib/share-panels`) and parses a panel's text and widget once per change. `cursor` is true in the hangar and while the battle cursor is shown; `edit` is the edit modifier held in the hangar and the cursor shown in battle, and only then can a draggable panel take the mouse; `hover` is true in battle. Without OpenWG Gameface there is no renderer (`HUD: OpenWG Gameface: ..., battle and hangar panels are off`): every stock element stays and the features fall back to system messages. Without the inject classes (`InjectComponentAdaptor`, wulf `ViewImpl`, the entities factory: `core/client/inject.page_usable`) only the hangar panels are off (`HUD: the client has no inject adaptor for the hangar page (...)`). A window that fails to open marks it broken, and the battle panels stay off for the rest of the session.
  - **The hangar page (`core/client/hud/inject_page`, spec phase 1):** `core/client/inject.InjectHost` creates the client's own `GFInjectComponent` through the app's AS3 `ClassFactory`, adds it to the view's display list at 0,0 and registers our `PageInjectAdaptor` for it (`registerFlashComponent`), which adds the page as a wulf child view of the main window (not a window: no keyboard focus of its own, no layer); the page's `fitView` sizes it to the client. `ViewWatch` places it on every `loaderManager.onViewLoaded` of the hangar view (`otmetkiHudInject`, and on one already loaded) in the lobby app; nothing is placed in the battle app. The page leaves with the hangar view (research, the store, the queue, the battle) and comes back with it, and a view reloaded before the old one went moves it (one page, no duplicate). It starts with the first label of the session. The GFInjectComponent's `mouseEnabled`/`mouseChildren` are on only while the player holds the edit modifier, so every other click, the hangar camera's drag included, goes to the hangar; while editing the page's own input area decides. **When a page fails** (no ClassFactory, an error placing it, or not loaded within `HUD_INJECT_LOAD_TIMEOUT_S`, 10 s) it is taken out and logged (`HUD: the page could not be placed in the hangar view, its panels stay off until it loads again (the stock HUD keeps its elements)`); nothing else draws its panels, the page confirms nothing drawn, so every stock element stays, and the next load of the view tries again. `python.log`: `HUD: the hangar panels are drawn inside the hangar view`, `inject: watching the SF_LOBBY app for hangar`, `inject otmetkiHudInject: GFInjectComponent placed in hangar (layout N)`, `inject otmetkiHudInject: page loaded`, `HUD: Gameface page ready in the hangar view (N labels: …)`, and in battle `HUD: Gameface window N opened in the battle (layout N)`, `HUD: Gameface page ready in the HUD window (…)`.
  - **None:** `NullBackend` keeps every panel hidden; the battle summary still works (system messages).
- **Settings UI:** a settings window reads `hud_layer(app).schemas` / `.panels` and writes through `hud_layer(app).update_settings(panel_id, values)` (a shown panel moves at once).
- **Widgets (protocol v3):** `show(panel_id, text, widget)` also carries a structured payload `{kind, v, data}` (`core/hud/widget`); the Gameface page draws its own component for the kind (`ui-web/src/entities/hud/<kind>`: plates, Warhelios, icon + number rows, bars, SVG radial timers) and falls back to `text` when it does not know the kind or the data fails its zod schema. Icons are strings built in Python (`core/hud/icons`): client images by path (`img://gui/maps/icons/...`, RU 1.45 files) with our glyph after `|` as the fallback (`otmetki:<name>`, drawn inline by the page: fire, fall, ammo_rack, record, target, wn8, session, traverse, bush, mission, arty, points and the class/kind glyphs); the Gameface backend swaps a client file `ResMgr.isFile` does not find for the glyph before sending. Each feature's model writes its widget fixture into `ui-web/src/shared/api/hud-protocol/_tests/fixtures/widgets/` (`OTMETKI_UPDATE_FIXTURES=1`), which the page's tests parse and render. The shared plate `card` (`core.hud.widget.card`, `card_row`, `card_chip`; page `entities/hud-widgets/card`) draws every hangar label and the smaller battle panels (High Caliber, battle WN8, personal best, hits on you, death card, gun traverse, goals, personal missions): a caps header with an icon, a big value on the right, icon + number chips, rows of icon or status mark + text + value with an optional one-line dimmed detail and a thin progress bar, and a dimmed footer; 260 px wide by default, colours by role (`HUD_TONE_COLORS`, the dark theme tokens) or, for ratings, the rating scale's own colour. Glyphs are filled with explicit colours, never `currentColor`. Every plate has a left stripe coloured by category (`core.hud.widget.RAILS`, ui-web `HUD_RAILS`): `damage` orange (damage log, hit log), `incoming` red (hits on you, last hit, death card, arty meter, battle wounds), `progress` gold (marks, records, goals, missions, ratings, session), `info` grey (clock, equipment, traverse, hangar info, platoon). The client HUD font (Warhelios, RU 1.45) has no glyph above U+2000 but a short list of punctuation; the page runs every widget string and label through `shared/lib/font-safe` (U+2212 to `-`, narrow spaces to U+00A0, ≈ to `~`, arrows to ‹ ›, other symbols dropped) and `hud-format` writes ASCII `-` and U+00A0 itself. Counts with a word use `core.format.counted` (`2 боя`, `5 боёв`, `1 battle`).
- **Replacing stock elements (`core/hud/stock`, `core/client/hud/stock`):** a panel returns the `BATTLE_VIEW_ALIASES` it replaces from `stock_aliases()` (team_hp: `fragCorrelationBar`, damage_log: `battleDamageLogPanel`, sixth_sense: `sixthSense`). They are hidden only while the panel runs and the Gameface page confirms the panel on the screen (whenever the page comes up after the panel started, with no new update needed): it sends `{type: 'drawn', ids}` with the panels it laid out at a non-zero size whenever that set changes (`HudLayer.draws`), so a panel not yet measured, never shown or on a page that is gone leaves the stock element on screen (a lamp, `BattlePanel.stock_while_hidden` (sixth_sense), needs only the page up, since the stock lamp lights with ours): the wrapped `SharedPage._setComponentsVisibility` moves them from `visible` to `hidden` on `ClassicPage` (and its subclasses) in the battle types that replace stock elements only (see the layout per battle type below), so the page's own re-shows (control mode, Tab, postmortem) never bring them back; switching the component off or the page not drawing the panel gives them back at once (`as_setComponentsVisibilityS`, or the page's full-stats set while Tab is open). Our battle panels follow the stock GUI without leaving the page, by one rule (`HudLayer.set_cover(reason, on)`, `core/hud/layer/constants.py` `COVER_REASONS`) fed by one watch, `core/client/hud/cover` (every overlay, its signal and why: [docs/research/client/2026-10-05-battle-overlays.md](../../../docs/research/client/2026-10-05-battle-overlays.md)): `GameEvent.GUI_VISIBILITY` (V), the battle loading screen with the team lists (`GameEvent.BATTLE_LOADING`), the battle page hiding its own HUD (the wrapped `SharedPage._setComponentsVisibility` hides `teamBasesPanel`, the XVM reference, under the full stats on any tab: Tab, the personal missions and personal reserves keys; or shows the full stats, the event stats, a respawn screen or the Frontline overview map) and a full-screen Gameface window (`core/client/hud/cover/windows`) make the shown panels `visible: false`. The Esc menu, the F1 help and dialogs are left alone, as the stock HUD, Battle Observer and XVM stay under them. The HUD window stays on `WindowLayer.WINDOW`: the full stats and the loading screen are components of the Scaleform battle page (`VIEW`), so no wulf layer lies under them and over the page's own panels (`core/client/hud/gameface/constants.py`). Each source reports its whole current state, so stacked overlays never flicker the panels; while anything covers the watch checks the client every second, so a missed close never keeps them hidden; the companion switch `hud_hide_under_windows` (on) turns the game windows off and leaves V and the loading screen. The post-mortem camera on the killer covers nothing: the stock HUD stays on it and so do our panels (as Battle Observer's and XVM's), so the stock elements they replace stay hidden. The stock suppression follows the layer (`HudLayer.watch`, `releases_stock`): a panel muted by the streamer mode, blocked or left out of the battle type gives its stock elements back meanwhile. The page keeps a hidden panel mounted and laid out in its dock at opacity 0 (no drag, no click). No panel is deleted and created again, so the page keeps its measured size and place and nothing jumps when Tab or V is released. `stock_aliases()` may also name parts of the stock reticle (`core.hud.stock.RETICLE_PARTS`, the opacity keys of the crosshair panel's settings): the crosshair readouts name, from the payload the page got, the reload timer (and the magazine indicator while the box draws the magazine: `drum_style` not `off`, or any style on an auto-reloader, whose stock indicator, RU 1.45 `CrosshairBase.autoloaderComponent` at the cassette opacity, carries its own reload timer, so its box draws the magazine as shells; a magazine count the client cannot answer for a moment, `getCurrentShells` at `SHELL_QUANTITY_UNKNOWN` after a setup change or a shell switch, keeps the magazine last drawn) for a drawn reload box, the zoom indicator for a drawn zoom (`show_zoom`, the sniper view only) and the reload and HP indicators for each drawn arc; a box with nothing to show (no reload from the client yet, an unknown reload time) names nothing. The box stays on screen (the full reload time while the gun is loaded, as the stock timer) in the arcade, sniper and strategic reticles. `core/client/hud/stock/reticle.py` wraps `CrosshairPanelContainer.setSettings` (installed with the stock control, when the first battle panel is created in the hangar: the crosshair panel takes its first settings during the loading screen, before `battle_ready`) and hands the panel those parts at opacity 0 while they are wanted, and pushes the player's own settings back (`as_setSettingsS` with the last settings the panel got) the moment they are not: switched off, another camera mode, the camera on an ally, the battle page left. The saved client settings are never written. The competitor conventions per stock element are in [docs/research/competitors/2026-10-05-stock-replacement.md](../../../docs/research/competitors/2026-10-05-stock-replacement.md).
- **Layout per battle type (`core/hud/modes`, `core/client/hud/modes`, feature `hud_layouts`):** a battle panel's start asks the arena for its type: `ARENA_GUI_TYPE` first (`BigWorld.player().arena.guiType`, RU 1.45 `client_common/ClientArena.py`), then `bonusType`, then the battle page's `VIEW_ALIAS`: `random` (random, training, ranked, Mapbox, clan, general battle), `comp7` (Onslaught and its tournament, gui 30/33), `frontline` (EPIC_BATTLE 21/22), `battle_royale` (Steel Hunter, 23) and `event` (event battles, fun random, maps training, story mode 100, space 300, Waffenträger `white_tiger` 301, any extension id from 100). The layer (`HudLayer.enter_mode`) asks the policy `hud_layouts` set (`set_policy`) which panels the type shows (`full`, `compact` = marks and damage log, `off`) and whether it keeps places of its own; a panel outside the set does not start, so it replaces nothing. A drag in a battle of a type other than random is saved for that type in components.json `hud_layout_places` (`ModePlaces`; random keeps the panels' own settings), and `Reset layout` clears it. Without the feature every panel shows everywhere at its own place, as before. A stock element is replaced on every battle page that has it (any `SharedPage`: random, ranked, Onslaught, Frontline, Waffenträger and the other events, Steel Hunter), looked up by its alias in `page.components` and re-checked as the page registers its components, never by the battle type; the HUD report names the page and the stock aliases found and hidden.
- **Mouse in battle:** the page limits its view's input with `viewEnv.setInputArea` (nothing outside an edit). The area is cut to the view in whole design px (a panel flush with the bottom edge, as the marks, reached past it at a fractional design size such as 3840x2160 at 1.75, and the engine then gave the page no press there). «Nothing» is one pixel in the top-left corner, never an empty area (UNVERIFIED on Lesta 1.45 how the engine reads a 0×0 area), and the page sets the area again every second (`HUD_OVERLAY.inputAreaRefreshMs`) and after a refused call, because the view is resized to the client after the first area was set and the engine may reset it with the view. It reports `{type: 'area', whole}` when the area turns into the whole screen or back, and `otmetki.log` gets one line per change: `HUD: the page takes the mouse over the whole screen (lobby, editing)` / `... only over the panel it edits (...)`. In a hangar edit it takes the whole screen; in battle (state `hover`) it polls the cursor (`viewEnv.getMouseGlobalPositionPx`, every 50 ms while the cursor is shown) and takes only the movable panel under it (or a widget with tooltips, `defineHudWidget({ pointer: true })`), and the whole screen while a panel is dragged, so the cursor still reaches the minimap, the team lists and the chat; the CSS `pointer-events` stay as a second layer. The page reports once per page the first pointer over a panel, press and wheel turn (`{type: 'mouse', event}`), and `otmetki.log` gets `HUD: battle cursor shown, panels can be dragged`, `HUD: the page saw the mouse in edit mode (…)`, or at the end of the battle `the battle cursor was shown, but the page saw no mouse over a panel`. **Keyboard focus:** the hangar page is a child view, not a window: it takes no keyboard focus, so the lobby channels and Alt+Tab keep working. The battle's HUD window never keeps it (the page has no text field). The engine owns the wulf focus and gave it to the HUD window unasked when the focused window went away (the 1.45 live log; the hangar then took no click and the chat no key until the game was minimised), so the backend hands every focus it gets on a frame later with `tryFocus()` back to the window that had it before (`core/client/hud/gameface/last_focus`: every wulf window's `_cFocusChanged`, Scaleform's `SFWindow` pages included, so the battle chat keeps typing), or, when that window is gone or hidden, to the topmost shown window on the page and dialog layers (`VIEW`…`OVERLAY`, no tooltip), or the main window, as the client corrects the engine itself (`fade_manager._bringToFront`); while a panel is being dragged (the cursor shown) it waits until the cursor goes, and after the client gives the focus back three times within half a second it tries again a second later (`core/hud/focus`). `otmetki.log` gets one line per hand-on: `HUD: Gameface window N took the focus in the battle, handing it to <window>`. A hold of the edit modifier starts when BigWorld reports the key down and Windows (`GetAsyncKeyState`) agrees, and then lasts while Windows reports the physical key down, polled every `RELEASE_POLL_S` (`core/client/hud/modifier`): BigWorld keeps Alt down when its key-up went to another window (Alt+Tab), which would keep the page over the whole hangar, and drops every key when its window loses the keyboard focus. While the battle cursor is shown its visibility is polled (`CURSOR_POLL_S`), so a cursor hidden without an event no longer keeps a panel tooltip open at the screen centre, over the reticle.
- **Places across resolutions and interface scales:** a place is an offset from a named anchor (`align_x`/`align_y`: a screen edge or the centre) in design px, which the page multiplies by the interface scale (1 rem = 1 design px), so a panel keeps its distance from its edge after a resolution or scale change; the page clamps every panel to the screen when it lays it out, and a drop saves the place against the nearest anchor (the screen thirds), so the offset stays small. A saved offset that points past its own edge (negative from the left or top, positive from the right or bottom) is brought back to that edge when the panel registers (`core/hud/panel.fit_place`). A press becomes a drag only after 5 px (`HUD_OVERLAY.clickSlop`, `HUD_EDITOR.dragSlop`), and a drag is saved once, on mouse-up, in battle and in the settings window's HUD editor alike (the editor sends no `hud_move` while the mouse moves).
- **Old default places:** components.json keeps every default it was written with, so each panel's schema names the places older versions gave it (`panel_schema(..., retired=RETIRED_PLACES)`); a panel still at one of them (never moved) goes to today's default when it registers (`core/hud/panel.retired_reset`). A panel with a `pinned` key that is on (team HP, the equipment row) stays at its default place and takes no drag (`pinned_values`); the player can switch it off in the panel's card.
- Components start on the app's `battle_ready` (own battles, never replays; the clock on `battle_enter`) and hide on `battle_leave`: each panel is a `core/client/hud/panel.BattlePanel` (`start`, `stop`, `render`; the base registers the panel, its `HudPreview` and the lifecycle). Battle-session events are subscribed through its `hooks` (`core/client/battle.BattleHooks`), which retries for 20 s while the session is not ready and unsubscribes everything on leave; tick loops use `core/client/timer.Ticker` (a countdown subtracts `ticker.elapsed()`, the game time since the previous tick, never the interval).

### marks_panel — MoE panel in battle

- **What:** a `BattlePanel` (`marks_panel`, movable in the HUD editor, preview in the edit mode): the dossier percent at the start of the battle, the projection after it and the change, the combined damage this battle (damage + the best of radio, track and stun assist) and the average before and after, the damage still needed this battle for 65/85/95% (✓ once reached; 100% when it is next), and the damage for +`step` percent. The maths are `core/moe`. The battles to the next mark, the trend, the history and the tank's silhouette are the Tank card's (below): in battle the panel keeps to this battle, as the gunmarks panels and XVM's marks macros do.

- **In battle, «Итоги прошлого боя в бою» (`last_battle` panel, switch `battle_last_results`, on):** results of an earlier battle that reach the client during the next one (RU 1.45 `Avatar.receiveBattleResults` fires `onBattleResultsReceived` in battle) show by themselves as a compact card for 8 s (`LAST_SHOW_S`): map, tank, outcome, damage, XP, credits after costs and the MoE change (the summary's results-scale percent, `core.moe.results_rating`). One at a time, up to five waiting; nothing to click. The page slides the card in from the right and fades it out over its `show_s`. Default place, as the established packs show their results notice: right aligned 8 px from the right edge, 12 px above the minimap of the player's size (`attach` `minimap_above`). Only where the stock battle notifier does not show the same results (it shows them on the left where the battle type has it, the server enables it and the game option «enableBattleNotifier» is on; RU 1.45 `battle_shared/battle_notifier.py`, `battle_notifier_ctrl.py`): the panel reads the three once per battle (`session_provider().dynamic.battleNotifier`, `ILobbyContext.getServerSettings().isBattleNotifierEnabled()`, the settings core's `enableBattleNotifier`), logs the decision, and a condition it cannot read counts as off, so our card shows rather than nothing. No card shows the battle being played: after a death the stock post-mortem panel and the stock damage log totals carry it, at the end the stock results screen and the post-battle message (the former `battle_summary` card and switch are gone; config revision 4 drops their leftovers).
- **Widget:** the card is the `battle_summary` widget (`ui-web/src/entities/hud/battle-summary`); the text fallback draws the same lines.

## Battle extras

Components that change no panel: each is a feature package with a config.json switch (on by default) and a components.json section, active only in the player's own battles (never replays).

### chat_filter — battle chat

- **What:** a `[HH:MM:SS]` stamp on every chat line (team, common, squad; the epic-battle channel formats its own lines and keeps no stamp), and hiding of repeats (the same line from the same player within 30 s), flood (more than `rate_limit` lines per player within 10 s), quick-command spam (the same limit) and lines with a blocked word. The player's own lines are never hidden. Hidden lines are left out of the replay's chat as well.
- **Fair play:** it only hides or annotates chat the client already received. A hidden line skips the client's own `addMessage` entirely, so it is also left out of the channel history and of the replay's chat (`g_replayCtrl.onBattleChatMessage`); that is intended: the replay shows the chat as the player saw it.
- **Switch:** `battle_chat_filter`. **Section `chat_filter`:** `timestamp_format` (none, `%H:%M`, `%H:%M:%S`), `filter_duplicates`, `rate_limit` (0..20, 0 = off), `filter_commands`, `block_words` (comma-separated, case-insensitive).

### auto_messages — battle chat auto messages

- **What:** a line to the team (or the platoon) chat on the player's own events, the way kurzdor's «AutoBattleMessages» does ([spec](../../../docs/specs/2026-10-07-auto-messages.md)): an enemy SPG damaged the own tank (`arty_hit`, «Арта ({vehicle}) отстрелялась! По мне :O»), an ally damaged it (`team_damage`), the sixth sense lamp lit while at most `spotted_allies` (5) allies are alive (`spotted`, optionally followed by the stock «Нужна помощь!» or «Внимание на точку» command at the own tank); these three are on, as in kurzdor's mod. Off until turned on: ammo rack, crew member, track, fire and fire out, low HP (once), ramming, own kill, own damage milestone (once), last alive in the team (once), a long own reload, the greeting when the countdown ends and «gg» when the round ends. Each trigger has its text: variants split by `|`, one picked at random, placeholders `{tank}`, `{hp}`, `{damage}`, `{frags}` and the trigger's own (`{vehicle}`, `{hit}`, `{allies}`, `{enemies}`, `{seconds}`, `{percent}`, `{result}`); an empty text uses the built-in Russian or English variants. Only the battle chat's text smileys (`:)`, `:(`, `:O`, `>:O`, `;)`), never Unicode emoji.
- **How:** the chat input's own call, `BattleLayout.sendMessage` on the team or squad controller of `MessengerEntry.g_instance.gui.channelsCtrl`, after its `canSendMessage()` (the player's battle chat setting, teammates, Battle Royale, the 0.5 s broadcast cooldown); a refused line is dropped. Per-trigger cooldowns (30–60 s, or once a battle), at least `min_interval_s` (10 s) between two lines and at most 4 a minute; a line that would break them is dropped, never queued. A `ChatBanError` from the server stops the component for the battle. Never in a replay, never while the camera follows an ally, only in the `BATTLE` period (the «gg» as the round ends). Lines are cut to the client's 140 characters.
- **Fair play:** only the player's own events the client shows: the received-damage line of the stock damage log (the attacker and its class, nothing more), the damage panel's devices, fire and HP, the lamp, the own reload, kills and dealt damage, the team lists' alive counts.
- **Switch:** `battle_auto_messages` (on; only the arty, ally-damage and spotted lines start on). **Section `auto_messages`:** `channel` (`team`, `squad`), `min_interval_s` (3..60), per trigger `<id>` and `<id>_text`, `spotted_allies` (1..15), `spotted_extra` (none, `help`, `attention`), `low_hp_percent` (10..60), `damage_milestone_value` (500..10000), `reload_min_s` (5..60). A profile code never carries the switch or the section (a stranger's code must not post lines in the player's name).

### pack_badge — the Three Marks badge by names

Installed by the Recommended and Streamer presets, switch `battle_pack_badge` on by default. Marks players who use the modpack the way Near_You and Battle Observer draw in the stock battle UI: our own AS3 library SWF adds a plate (the «///» mark and the «ТРИ ОТМЕТКИ» wordmark) after the vehicle icon and an orange gradient behind the row in the players panel («уши»), the Tab stats and the loading screen. History: 0.3.7 used the stock prefix badge slot and 0.3.8 an `<IMG>` in the row's `region`; the owner's client crashed natively (0xC0000005) 0.5-3 minutes after either entered the rows, so 0.3.8 switched the badge off, and 0.1.3 drops both data paths for the SWF and switches it back on once (companion config revision 10, `ONE_TIME_SWITCHES`) (docs/specs/2026-10-06-modpack-user-badge.md).

- **Who:** every player with the mod installed; no binding and no site account are involved. The player himself locally (`show_pack_badge` on, the whole mod on; the own account id from the arena data), so the own badge shows when the site does not answer; others from the anonymous, unsigned `POST /mod/badges/presence` `{account_id, visible, account_ids}` (`core.client.me.post_json`) at `battle_ready` (never in a replay): the own account id, `visible` = `show_pack_badge` (and the whole mod on), the ids of the arena's players without the own account, bots and anonymised players (`fakeName` set), at most 100. The site records the own presence (a 30-day key per account, deleted on `visible: false`) and answers the subset that has the mod and shows the badge; the answer is kept for the battle only; a 429 carries Retry-After and the battle stays unmarked. Contract: [contract/badges.schema.json](contract/badges.schema.json).

- **Own switch:** «Показывать мой значок «Три отметки» другим игрокам» (`show_pack_badge`, on; a companion config key shown on this component's page through its `SETTINGS`), a mod setting only: it travels as `visible` in the battle's presence request (`false` while the whole mod is off), nothing else reports it. In AS3 `BadgePainter` covers three screens (RU 1.45 client source: `battle.swf`): the players panel (`playersPanel`, or `epicRandomPlayersPanel` on the epic random page; `listLeft`/`listRight` and their private `_items` of `BasePlayersPanelList`, each holder's public `getListItem()` and `vehicleID` from `BasePlayersListItemHolder`; the plate goes into the row, the strip just above the row backgrounds), the Tab table (`fullStats.tableCtrl`, protected in `StatsBase`, its protected `allyRenderers`/`enemyRenderers` from `StatsTableControllerBase` with the public `containsData` and `getVehicleID()`; the icon, name and frags come from the public cell collections of `fullStats.statsTable` (`vehicleIconCollection`, `playerNameCollection`, `fragsCollection`) at `column * numRows + row`, the index `StatsTableItem` reads them by; the plate also clears the frags column next to the icon) and the loading screen (`battleLoading.form._allyRenderers`/`_enemyRenderers`, private in `BattleLoadingForm`, each renderer's protected `model.vehicleID` from `BasePlayerItemRenderer`; the icon and name from the public vectors `vehicleIconsAlly`/`vehicleIconsEnemy` and `textFieldsAlly`/`textFieldsEnemy` of the form's child `container`, a `BaseRendererContainer`, at the renderer's index). ### streamer_mode — streamer mode

- **What:** a hotkey (`ctrl_shift_h` by default; `ctrl_shift_s`, `f9`, `f10`, `f11` or none) takes every panel of the mod and every hangar label off the screen and brings them back with their latest text (`HudLayer.set_muted`, `Ui.set_muted`: the features keep updating, the layer holds the texts); `keep_hidden` keeps them off in the next battle. The private mode (`private`) hides the battle chat of other players (`BattleLayout.addMessage/addCommand`, the own lines stay) and blocks the hangar labels with the player's own numbers (ratings, session, goals, personal missions, marks history).
- **Fair play:** hides only; reads nothing.
- **Left out:** hiding the name and clan in the game's own interface (the players panel, the carousel header, the battle loading screen) needs patching Scaleform views; there is no verifiable Python API for it.
- **Switch:** `streamer_mode`. **Section `streamer_mode`:** `hotkey`, `keep_hidden`, `private`, `hide_chat`, `hide_hangar_stats`.

### bush_circle — the 15 m circle

- **What:** a 15 m circle on the ground around the own tank (the bush rule: farther than 15 m from the bush the shot reveals the tank), by a hotkey (`ctrl_shift_b` by default; `ctrl_shift_c`, `f7`, `f8` or none) or always (`mode`), in `white`, `green`, `yellow` or `cyan`; removed when the own tank is destroyed and at the end of the battle. The radius is the game's rule, not a setting.
- **How:** the game's own ground marker (RU 1.45 `CombatSelectedArea`): a `BigWorld.PyTerrainSelectedArea` with `content/Interface/CheckPoint/CheckPoint.visual`, 0.5 m over the terrain, on an empty `BigWorld.Model` added to the player and driven by `BigWorld.Servo(<own vehicle>.matrix)`, so it follows the tank without a tick. UNVERIFIED on Lesta 1.45: the visual and the servo on the vehicle matrix (live checklist item 12). On the minimap: left out, the minimap is a Scaleform view with no Python API for our own shapes.
- **Fair play:** the own tank's position and a fixed radius; nothing about other vehicles. A 15 m circle is not in Lesta's forbidden list (research 2026-09-29 §0).
- **Switch:** `battle_bush_circle`. **Section `bush_circle`:** `mode` (`hotkey`, `always`), `hotkey`, `color`.

### aim_info — aim and shells

- **Distance to any target:** the stock reticle shows the distance to the vehicle under it only when that vehicle's marker shows none (RU 1.45 `crosshair/plugins.TargetDistancePlugin.__shouldTrackVehicle`, which follows the marker settings); with `target_distance` on it answers yes for every vehicle, so the reticle distance follows every focused target, allies and enemies alike. The number is the client's own `avatar_getter.getDistanceToTarget`, the one the markers show.
- **Shell tooltips:** `shell_tooltips` extends the stock tooltip of the ammo slots (`ConsumablesPanel._makeShellTooltip`): the module damage (`descriptor.damage[1]`) goes at the end of its body, and when the client's technical info is off (a header only) the damage, penetration (the gun's piercing power at 100 m), shell speed (m/s, over the client's projectile speed factor) and module damage are added. The tooltip is built when the slots are added, so a change applies in the next battle.
- **Fair play:** the distance the client already measures for its markers, the player's own shells and the player's own reticle. Nothing about enemies beyond what the client shows. No armour under the reticle: Lesta's support article names «in-battle armour analysis» as a forbidden category (research 2026-09-29 §0), so the readout of the effective armour, the penetration and the hit angle (0.1.x) was removed with its settings; a stored `armor_under_aim` and the panel's place leave `components.json` by themselves (`DROPPED_KEYS`, defaults revision 4). The smaller aim circle moved out (revision 6 of the companion config moves a circle the player had on; today it is the `aim_circle` component).
- **Switch:** `battle_aim_info` (on). **Section `aim_info`:** `target_distance` (on), `shell_tooltips` (on). No HUD panel.

### responsive_reticle — the gun marker every frame

- **What:** the stock rotator (RU 1.45 `VehicleGunRotator`) turns the own gun and moves the client gun marker once per server tick (`SERVER_TICK_LENGTH`, 0.1 s), gliding it (and the turret and gun models) over the next 0.1 s, so the marker trails the mouse. A `Ticker` of 1 ms (every frame; due after the camera's own `delayCallback(0.0)` update, so it should read this frame's shot point — UNVERIFIED: the order BigWorld fires callbacks due in one frame) runs the same client prediction between the ticks: `__rotate(shotPoint, timeDiff)` with the frame's time, then `__updateGunMarker(relaxTime)`, and stamps the rotator's `__time`, so the stock tick still sends the aim to the server (`__updateShotPointOnServer`, before its own time check) at 10 Hz and skips the turning it no longer needs. `follow`: `instant` (default) lands the marker at once (relax 0.001 s, the stock's own instant relax in a replay time warp; a relax as long as the frame left the marker drawn a frame late), `smooth` relaxes over 50 ms (a lag of its own). During a frame's turn the rotator's `__ROTATION_TICK_LENGTH` is set on the instance to the frame (or the smooth relax), so the turret and gun models glide over the frame and keep up with the marker; it is removed right after.
- **One writer:** a stock tick that still turns (its 20 ms `__INSUFFICIENT_TIME_DIFF` passed, i.e. under 50 fps or after a hitch) goes through `updateRotationAndGunMarker`, which the component overrides: the turn runs with the component's relax and caches instead of the stock 0.1 s relax for one frame (a visible hitch every tick), and the frame after it sees no time to turn.
- **Still frames:** a frame whose shot point and own vehicle (`getAvatarOwnVehicleStabilisedMatrix`: position, yaw, pitch, roll) are within 1e-5 of the last frame's while the last turn moved neither yaw nor pitch does no work (no turn, no collision trace, no marker update) and leaves the stock tick to glide the marker as before; any move takes over again on the next frame.
- **Throttled per server tick:** the dispersion (`PlayerAvatar.getOwnVehicleShotDispersionAngle`, called by `__rotate`; stateful, it moves the aiming timer) is worked out once per tick while a frame runs and blended from the last value handed out to the new one over the tick (`TickBlend`), the way the stock marker glides its size over a tick, so the circle shrinks smoothly instead of in ten steps a second; the stock penetration colour (`ShotResultIndicatorPlugin.__onGunMarkerStateChanged`, a collision walk over the target's armour) runs for the first marker update of each tick only. Another component's hook on `_DefaultGunMarkerController.update` (the smaller aim circle, `aim_circle`) runs once per marker update with the same relax time.
- **Realms:** the `__rotate` signature is checked against the realm's (`constants.CURRENT_REALM` `RU` is Lesta: `(shotPoint, timeDiff)`); UNVERIFIED on WG: the same two names are expected, any other signature keeps the component off with a python.log line.
- **Off:** for SPGs (their marker is the strategic one), in replays, and on guns with `staticTurretYaw` (no horizontal traverse).
- **Fair play:** the drawing of the own marker from the client's own prediction of the own gun; the aim sent to the server, the shot and the dispersion are the stock ones; nothing about other vehicles is read.
- **Switch:** `battle_responsive_reticle` (on: drawing only). **Section `responsive_reticle`:** `follow` (`instant`, `smooth`).

### battle_hotkeys — the game's option keys in battle

- **What:** two hotkeys toggle two of the game's own options right in battle: the server reticle (`useServerAim`, `server_aim_key`, `ctrl_shift_j` by default) and the extended zoom x16/x25 (`increasedZoom`, `zoom_key`, `ctrl_shift_k`; `ctrl_shift_n`, `ctrl_shift_m` or none for either). The option is written the way the game's settings window writes it (`core.client.native.apply_settings`); the rotator and the sniper camera follow the change at once. A notice (widget `option_notice`) over the reticle says the new state for 2 seconds.
- **Why in battle:** a key press is the player's own change of a game option, the same as the settings window in battle; nothing is written on its own (README «Client settings» rule).
- **Fair play:** only the game's own options; nothing beyond what the game's settings window offers.
- **Switch:** `battle_hotkeys` (off: it changes the player's game settings). **Section `battle_hotkeys`:** `server_aim_key`, `zoom_key`, the notice's place.

## In-game UI

Package `packages/ui` (`net.triotmetki.ui`, depends on core and companion; registers through the core registry as `ui`, so it attaches in any load order). Without OpenWG Gameface it logs one line and `config.json` is the only way to change settings.

- **HUD page:** `hud.html` (ui-web `src/hud.tsx`, view `views/hud`) is the Gameface HUD renderer's page, registered as `otmetki/ui/hud` in `res_map/net.triotmetki.ui.json`; the lobby draws it inside the Scaleform hangar view, the battle in one transparent full-screen HUD window (one page, one protocol: «The HUD layer», Renderer). It draws each label from its anchor in rem (1rem = 1px of the design, the layout's coordinates) as a left/top rect on the client screen in design pixels (`viewEnv.getClientSizePx()` over the interface scale, `viewEnv.remToPx(1)` or else the root font size; `entities/hud/panel-layout` `readScreen`), clamped so the whole panel stays on screen at any resolution and interface scale (`lib/anchor` `placeRect`, tested over 1280×720…3840×2160 and scale 1–2); it sizes its root (and, when the client has `viewEnv.resizeViewPx`, the view, every time) to the client, reads the screen once `engine.whenReady` resolves and again on the engine's `clientResized` and `self.onScaleUpdated` events and on `resize` (the OpenWG Gameface convention, no poll), keeps each label's widest measured size while its line count stays (a ticking clock does not shift a right-anchored panel), and hides a label until it is measured: Gameface lays the page out in its own frame, so a label inserted or changed this render may still read 0×0 or its old size, and the page reads the sizes again on the next few animation frames (`HUD_OVERLAY.measureFrames` after the last change) instead of waiting for the next state; renders the panels' HTML subset (`<font color size>`, `<b>`, `<i>`, `<u>`, `<br>`, `\n`, `<img src="img://...">`) through `shared/lib/rich-text` into spans, never as HTML, and lets the player move a panel only while the state's `edit` is true (the edit modifier held with a cursor) (`entities/hud/panel-layout`, the editor's anchor maths; the drag and the wheel resize are `features/hud/edit-panels`). The protocol is `core/hud/surface` on the Python side and `shared/api/hud-protocol` on the page; `packages/core/tests/test_hud_backends` writes the state fixture the page's zod schema parses and checks both command lists. The page itself is transparent and full screen, and the mouse passes through it except over a panel the player can drag (`src/shared/styles/hud.scss`). The panels of one docked column (`core/hud/panel` `DOCKS`) stack under each other in `order` (over each other for a bottom anchor); a top-anchored column taller than the room down to its group's reserve first moves up, not above `ceiling` nor into an undocked panel above it, and a panel that still crosses the reserve starts a new column beside the first, towards the middle of the screen (`lib/dock`). In edit mode the input area is the whole screen, so presses and wheel turns are read on the page and hit-tested against the panels' rects (`entities/hud/panel-layout` `lib/hit-panel`, the last drawn panel on top; `pointer` also counts a panel that only has tooltips): an icon, plate or SVG under the pointer would otherwise swallow the press or start the engine's own image drag. The `card` widget (`core/hud/widget`) is the shared plate of the hangar labels and the smaller battle panels. The Tank card has its own widget (`tank_card`). HUD colours for SVG fills and inline styles are hex values (`shared/config/hud-tones.constants.ts`, the redesign spec §4.4) because Gameface does not resolve `currentColor` reliably in inline SVG; they are the dark theme of `@otmetki/design-tokens` and `_tests/hud-tones.test.ts` keeps them equal. Every plate carries one left stripe colour per category (`shared/config/hud-rails.constants.ts`, spec §4.1: damage dealt, damage taken, progress, information); `core/hud/widget` `RAILS` lists the same names. `rich-text` avoids `matchAll`, `Object.hasOwn` and `Array#at`: Gameface runs an unknown V8 and the bundle lowers only syntax (es2017), not the runtime.
- **Window:** a Gameface `WindowImpl` + `ViewImpl` whose `ViewModel` has two string properties, `state` (the settings UI state as JSON) and `feed` (the large page the window shows, see the bridge below), one number property `escape` (the count of Esc presses the page is asked to step back from) and one command `send` (`{message: '<json>'}`). While it is open the client's own window blur covers the hangar behind it (`client/window/blur`: RU 1.45 `gui/shared/view_helpers/blur_manager.CachedBlur(enabled=True, ownLayer=window.layer - 1)`, `fini()` on close), so the page draws no backdrop of its own. The page is `ui-web` built into `packages/ui/gameface/index.html` (one self-contained file: styles inlined in the head, the script inlined as a classic IIFE `<script>` at the end of the body, the way the client's own Gameface pages load theirs; no ES module loader) and shipped at `res/gui/gameface/mods/triotmetki/ui/`; `res_map/net.triotmetki.ui.json` registers it for OpenWG Gameface (Lesta needs its Lesta-compatible build; the first start after install restarts the client once). The page registers the model the way the RU 1.45 bundles and OpenWG's `libs/model.js` do, `addDataChangedCallback('model', 0, true)`, and reacts only when that id is among the event's `callbackIDs`; `subViews` is native (`ids()`, `get(resId).model`). Both pages call `viewEnv.resizeViewPx` to fit the view to the client: a wulf view otherwise takes the size of its content, so a page whose root is `100%` of a 0×0 view never shows (Lesta 1.45.0.0 live test, python.log 2026-09-29: the HUD page, which called it, drew; the settings page, which did not, stayed invisible).
- **Entry point:** the window opens only from ModsList (`client/entry_points/mods_list`, `addModification(id='otmetki', icon='gui/gameface/mods/triotmetki/ui/icon.png', login=False, lobby=True)`; see [References and licences](#references-and-licences) for the 1.6.01 pin, a required dependency): the entry sits in the stock bottom-right row of the hangar. There is no HUD button and no hotkey; without ModsList the ui logs `ModsList (gui.modsListApi) not installed, the settings window has no entry`. The entry is lobby-only: the window's pages and actions need the hangar. The on-screen HUD edit mode («Расположение панелей») closes the window and ends with Esc (the lobby input manager's escape listener, `UiHost.edit_escape`, held only while the mode is on) or with a click on the ModsList entry, which opens the window again. The window never opens in battle: `UiHost.open` refuses while `app.in_battle`, and the window and the edit mode close on `battle_enter`.
- **Bridge (pure, `ui/bridge`):** `SettingsBridge.state()` and `handle(message)`; the glue only moves JSON. Messages: `ready`, `close`, `set {component, key, value}`, `set_many {component, values}` (a card's reset to its defaults and its undo, one revision), `action {component, action, row?, value?}`, `language`, `bind {code}`, `open {path}` (site-relative paths only, joined to the site next to `server_url`), `profile_save/load/rename/delete/export/import`, `hud_edit {active}`, `hud_move {panel, x, y, align_x?, align_y?}`, `hud_reset {panel}`, `window_layout {x, y, width, height, zoom, placed?}`, `feed {component, active}`, `escape` (quiet: the page took the last Esc itself), `scroll {page, top}` (quiet: where the player left a page; the bridge keeps it for the game session and sends it back as the state's `scroll`), `diag {text}` (quiet: one line `ui: page <text>` in otmetki.log, at most 400 characters; the page sends one per kind per open: the screen, scale and view rect it reads, the first press, drag, release, wheel turn and Esc). Protocol v2: a large page travels apart from the settings state (`ui/feeds`): the page sends `feed {component, active: true}` while it shows it (the replays page), and the bridge answers on the view model's second string property `feed` with a snapshot `{v, feed, rev, base: null, page, items}`, then only what changed, `{v, feed, rev, base, page, set, del}` (items by `id`); a delta whose `base` the page does not hold makes it ask again (`feed` active), which sends a new snapshot. The glue reads the feed at once after each of the player's messages and on the app's tick at most every 3 s (`FEED_INTERVAL_S`), sending nothing when nothing changed; `feed` is quiet (no new settings state), and the messages a single player message sets off (its own, `component_settings`, `config_changed`) become one state push, which the window skips when it equals the last one. The state carries `window` (`ui/window_layout`: the window's own `settings_window` section of components.json in design pixels, zoom 50–200 %, `placed: false` until the player moves it; never part of a profile or a profile code). A test checks the command list against `ui-web/src/shared/api/protocol`, and the page's zod schema parses a state fixture the Python tests write (`OTMETKI_UPDATE_FIXTURES=1` regenerates it).
- **Cards (`ui/components`):** the companion's data switches (`COMPANION_KEYS`) first, then every attached feature, then HUD panels no feature claims. Each card carries `section` (the window page: `battle`, `hangar`, `replays`, `data`, or `hud`: the cards the HUD editor's page lists under its panel map, `hud_layouts`; component pages follow where a component shows, the way the packs' configurators split battle and hangar, so the marks, the session and the streamer components sit on `battle` or `hangar`) and `context` (`hangar`, `battle` or `any`: where the component shows anything) from `components/constants.py` `PLACEMENT`; a component missing there falls back to its group (a panel or `battle` group → battle, `data` → data, else hangar). `catalog/catalog.json` carries the same `context` for every component (setupkit validates it and writes it into components.json; `packages/ui/tests/test_placement.py` keeps the two in step). A page row may carry `details: [{label, value}]`; the page shows them behind a «Подробнее» toggle (the battle summaries and the marks history use it). A card's switch is the feature's config.json switch (`settings.SETTINGS`); its fields come from its components.json section (`settings.SCHEMA`) or its companion keys; field type, limits and choices are derived from the `Schema` (bool, int with min/max, choice, text). Position keys (`x`, `y`, `align_*`, `drag`, `scale`) are left to the HUD editor; a HUD panel's `alpha` is folded (`PANEL_ADVANCED_KEYS`). A field the window should not offer is not hidden but removed: `core.settings.fix(schema, FIXED)` drops the key from the schema (it leaves the file on the next save) while `Settings.get` still answers the fixed value (docs/specs/2026-09-30-hud-consolidation-and-design.md section 12 lists every removed and folded field). `ADVANCED` in the feature's settings names the fields folded under «Дополнительно», the companion's own in `COMPANION_ADVANCED`. A feature instance may add buttons and a list page with duck-typed `ui_actions()`, `ui_page()` and `ui_action(action, row, value)` (the replay manager and hangar tweaks do); a page too large for every state push returns only its `kind` from `ui_page()` and the whole page from `ui_feed(poll)` (the replay manager: the folder is listed again on the player's request only, a poll reads what the background indexing added, and `ItemCache` builds an item again only when its file, header or marks change). A visual component adds `ui_editor()`, returning `{groups: [{id, label, keys}], icons: {key: {value: image}}, swatches: {key: {value: '#rrggbb'}}}`: its page then lays the fields out in those groups (ui-web `ComponentEditor`: the fields in their own scroll on the left, fields no group names last; the live preview with 1×/2× zoom and the hovered setting's hint on the right; a choice with `icons` is a thumbnail gallery and one with `swatches` colour swatches, both in fixed rows that fit the column; any other choice is one row of chips when its labels fit the column and a drop-down (ui-kit `Select`) when they would wrap; a text field sits under its label at the full width). Every card opens such a page, editor or not (no card expands in the list): without `ui_editor()` the fields form one «Настройки» group, a `gallery` becomes the gallery rows, and the page adds the card's buttons and list page under the hint. A search hit opens its page scrolled to the first matching setting, which is marked; «Назад» (or Esc) returns to the list at its scroll position. A feature need not write the hook: the window reads `editor(settings, translate)` from the feature's `model/editor.py` (built with `core.editor.editor_spec`: groups labelled `<id>_group_<group>`, plus `samples`, the real HUD widgets drawn as previews under their caption `<id>_sample_<sample>` (the Tank card, the Session card, the clock strip), and `schematic`, `minimap` or `camera`, a drawing of the stock element the window makes from the field values); a page that is only its field groups needs no module, `EDITOR_GROUPS` in the feature's `model/constants.py` is enough (`core.editor.groups_editor`). Advanced fields form the editor's last group, the preview switches between a «Лес» and a «Снег» backdrop. The crosshair, sixth sense, team HP, battle log, marks, equipment row, minimap, camera, session, clock strip, gun arc, aim info, aim circle, platoon points and battle progress have one. A card without an editor may show a summary: the chosen keys as key chips or the checked items as a list (ui-web `CARD_PREVIEWS`). Three more optional hooks, each sent only when the instance has it: `ui_thumb()` puts `thumb` on the card (an `img://` path or None: a picture of the current choice, `hangar_space`), `ui_gallery()` puts `gallery` on it (`{field key: {choice value: img:// path or None}}`: that choice field becomes a picture gallery, None tiles draw a styled fallback; `sixth_sense` icon sets), and `ui_advanced()` names field keys (any iterable) that get `advanced: true` (the other fields carry no `advanced` key); the window folds those under «Дополнительно». A list page may carry `layout: 'gallery'` (rows as picture tiles) and `note` (a line of text above the rows), and its rows `image` (an `img://` path or None); the bridge passes these through untouched. Group: `GROUP` in the feature's settings (`data`, `hangar`, `battle`). A feature that draws more than one thing lists the others in `PARTS` of its settings (`{id, switch, group, advanced, editor}`): each is a card of its own right after the feature's, with its components.json section, its config.json switch, its folded fields, the editor function named `editor` in the feature's `model/editor.py` and the instance `ui_parts()[id]` of the feature for its actions and page (the marks: the battle panel `marks_panel` and the Tank card `hangar_marks`).
- **Window layout (page):** a movable, resizable window over the blurred hangar (`widgets/window/window-frame`): drag the title to move it, drag the right or bottom edge or the corner grip to resize it (760×480 design px minimum, the screen maximum), double-click the title to centre it; zoom 80–150 % in the title bar scales the content (`transform: scale`) while the window keeps its size. It opens centred at its saved size. The page reads the screen in rem (`viewEnv.getClientSizeRem`), the scale (`remToPx(1)`) and where the client put its view (`getViewGlobalPositionRem`, `getViewSizeRem`): a WINDOW-flagged view need not start at the screen corner (1.45 live test: the box sat bottom-right and ran off the screen), so the box is placed and clamped on the part of the screen the view covers. Presses are read on the document and window and hit-tested against the handles' rects (`lib/hit`), never through the element under the pointer. Size and zoom are saved on release (`window_layout`). Esc steps back: the Python side holds Esc through the lobby app's `gameInputManager.addEscapeListener` while the window is open (`ui/escape` `EscapeGuard`, the way the client's Gameface dialogs keep Esc from its menu) and raises the model's `escape` number instead of closing; the page unwinds one layer per press (`shared/lib/escape-stack`: an open confirmation, then an open list or popover, then a focused text field, which clears a typed search or loses the focus) and answers `escape`, or `close` when nothing is left. `EscapeWatchdog` closes the window when the page stays silent for 0.6 s (`ANSWER_TIMEOUT_S`), and a press while the page is still loading closes it at once. The page itself never closes on an Esc keydown. Ctrl+F (by key code, any layout) puts the cursor in the search. The layout reflows by the width the content really gets: under 980 px the navigation collapses to icons, from 1360 px the cards sit in two columns. Title bar: the logo, search over component titles, hints, field labels and choices (from two letters; a result opens its component's page at the first matching setting), the binding chip (opens «Данные и сайт»), zoom, RU/EN, close. Left navigation: Бой, Ангар, Реплеи with on counts, then Профили, Редактор HUD and Данные и сайт (the site binding, not a component category). A page lists its cards sorted by title; a row of fixed height shows its thumbnail (the HUD sample, the card picture, or the component icon when it has neither), title, context badges when they differ from the page («Ангар», «Бой», «Панель HUD»), a one-line hint with an ellipsis, the number of settings changed from the default and its switch; a HUD panel without a switch of its own (`PANEL_OWNERS`: the last battle's summary) carries `owner` and its row shows «Часть» with the owner's title, which opens the owner's page. «Реплеи» is the replay browser at the full height of the page (the list and the details scroll on their own, the toolbar and the filters one row each, the totals as a status line under the list) with the page's components as compact header buttons (title opens the page, toggle switches it); a click opens its page with its settings (changed ones marked), the preview of a HUD panel with «Двигать на экране», its buttons and list page, and «Сбросить к стандартным». Every change applies at once and shows a toast with «Отменить» (up to 20 steps back, `entities/window/window-state` `$undo`). «Данные и сайт» starts with the account block: the binding state, the code field (with its placeholder drawn by the page, Gameface shows no `placeholder`), «Привязать», «Получить код» (`/mod` on the site) and «Открыть сайт».
- **Scrolling:** every scroll box is `overflow: hidden` with a `wheel` listener on the box itself (`shared/lib/wheel-scroll` `bindWheelScroll`, `shared/lib/use-wheel-scroll` for the replays page, built into the ui-kit `ScrollArea` with its draggable thumb): one step per notch, glided over 200 ms with an ease-out in whole pixels (`shared/lib/smooth-scroll`; a second notch extends the running glide, a thumb drag stops it), `deltaY > 0` scrolls down, an inner box at its end lets the wheel bubble to the outer one, the way the client's own Gameface scroll areas work (RU 1.45 `VerticalScroll`: `onWheel` on its area, `getDirection: deltaY > 1 ? Next : Prev`, `scrollTop` set by script). Native `overflow: auto` wheel scrolling ran backwards in the 1.45 client, and Gameface elements have no `hasAttribute`, so nothing inspects the tree to find the box (`mountOnce` keeps its mounted hosts in a `WeakSet` for the same reason). Gameface leaves `clientHeight` (and may leave `scrollHeight`) undefined, so every box is measured through `shared/lib/scroll-metrics`: the viewport from `clientHeight`, else `offsetHeight`; the content from `scrollHeight`, else the span of its children (`offsetTop` + `offsetHeight` of the last one minus the first one's `offsetTop`). Every page opens where the player left it during the game session (`useScrollMemory`, the `scroll` message).
- **HUD tooltips:** every label carries its component's short description, `hint` (`core.hud.panel.panel_hint`: the `component_<id>_hint` string of the component that draws it, `component_session_stats_hint` for `otmetki.session`). While the cursor is shown (battle: Ctrl; hangar: always) the page polls the pointer (`model/hooks/use-hovered-panel`) and asks the client for its own tooltip over the panel under it (`use-panel-hint`, the same `nativeTooltip` as the settings window); outside the client it draws a small plate of its own (`ui/components/HudHint`). A hint takes no input area, so clicks pass through a panel that cannot be dragged; the tooltip goes when the pointer leaves the panel, a drag starts or the cursor hides.
- **Tooltips and sounds:** the client draws the tooltips (`shared/api/gameface` `nativeTooltip`: `viewEnv.handleViewEvent({__Type: 'GFViewEventProxy', type: 1, contentID: R.views.common.tooltip_window.simple_tooltip_content.SimpleTooltipContent('resId'), decoratorID: …TooltipWindow('resId'), arguments: [GFValueProxy header, body]})`, as OpenWG Gameface's `mods/libs/views.js` and the client's own bundles send it; `shared/lib/use-tooltip`) and plays its UI sounds (`uiSound`: `engine.call('PlaySound', 'highlight' | 'play')` on a button's hover and click, bound once on the document by `shared/lib/ui-sounds`). Outside the client (the dev harness) a tooltip falls back to the `title` attribute and sounds are silent.
- **Glyphs:** the settings state's display texts go through `shared/lib/font-safe` (`entities/window/window-state/lib/font-safe-state`; ids, keys, values, links and names stay as sent): a character the client font lacks (★, →, ✓) is drawn with a look-alike instead of a blank. The client font (`gui/gameface/fonts/Warhelios-Regular.ttf` and `-Bold.ttf`, RU 1.45) has no glyph above U+2000 except the ones `font-safe.constants.ts` keeps (read from its cmap); a known character gets a look-alike, any other is dropped.
- **Icons:** the ui-kit `Icon` (`ui-kit/atoms/Icon`) draws icons from one sprite, `icons.png`, rendered at build time (`config/vite/icon-sprite`: lucide geometry read from `lucide-react` and the «///» mark from `@otmetki/icons/shapes`, rasterised with resvg, one block of rows per tone: muted, text, accent, contrast, success, danger) and shipped next to the pages; `shared/config/ui-icons.constants.ts` lists the names. The pages use no inline SVG and no rare glyphs (U+2212, U+00D7, U+00B1, U+03A3, U+202F came out blank in the 1.45 client).
- **Labels:** from the shared catalog, most specific first: `component_<id>` / `component_<id>_hint`; field `<id>_<key>`, `setting_<key>`, then the bare key (the companion labels its switches that way); hints with `_hint`; choices `<id>_<key>_<value>`, then `choice_<value>`. A feature adds these to its `i18n` STRINGS.
- **Profiles (`ui/profiles`):** `mods/configs/otmetki/profiles.json` `{version: 1, active, profiles: [{id, name, created, updated, data: {config, components}}]}`, at most 12. `data.config` is config.json without the connection and the privacy and network switches (`server_url`, `bind_code`, `settings_action`, `settings_target`, `settings_anonymous_stats`, `share_settings`, `upload_replays`, `publish_replays`, `send_*`, `settings_include_*`), which a profile or a code never carries or applies; `data.components` is the whole components.json (sections of components that are not installed included: they are stored as is and merged through their schema once installed). The manager reads and writes the same file. Profile codes `TM1.<base64url(zlib(json))>` copy a profile between players. **Site sync is not wired:** the settings-share contract (`contract/settings.schema.json`) is a strict whitelist of standard client settings and excludes mods by design, so syncing profiles to the site needs its own contract and endpoint.
- **HUD edit mode:** the window's editor draws the screen (the client size from `viewEnv.getClientSizePx()`) with every registered panel from `hud_layer(app).panels`; dragging (or the arrow keys) sends `hud_move` with the nearest anchor (`align_x`/`align_y` by screen third), throttled to 150 ms (remeda `funnel`, the last position after a pause included), and the bridge writes it through `hud_layer(app).update_settings`, so a shown panel moves live. «Edit on screen» emits `hud_edit(True)` on the bus and closes the window: every HUD panel (damage log, hit log, clock, team HP, sixth sense) shows itself with preview data in the hangar when its switch is on, and the player drags it while holding the edit modifier (`hud_modifier`); `hud_edit(False)` (hotkey, window reopened, battle) hides the previews, and a panel's own battle start ends its preview. `hud_describe(collect)` asks panels for the editor's miniature and the cards' previews: `collect(panel_id, preview=None, width=None, height=None, enabled=False, widget=None)`. A panel answers both through `core.hud.HudPreview(layer, panel_id, render_preview, is_enabled, can_show, size, render_widget).attach(app.bus)`; its preview text and widget come from the feature's pure `model/preview.py`. The state's `hud.panels[]` carry `text` (the rich preview) and `widget`, and the cards («Как выглядит в игре») and the editor's panels draw them with the HUD page's own widgets (`features/hud/widget-registry` `HudSample`, the widget registry both pages share), centred and scaled down to fit their frame (the ui-kit `FitBox`).
- **Look:** the site's design v4 tokens from `@otmetki/design-tokens` (`packages/design-tokens`, the same SCSS maps the site emits as CSS variables), dark theme. Each component has its own SCSS module (`<Component>.module.scss`, classes named `otmetki-<Component>__<class>`); `token(name)` from `src/shared/styles/_gameface.scss` inlines the static value, so the CSS carries no `var()`. Lengths are written in px and shipped as rem (`postcss-pxtorem`, 1rem = 1px of the design, because Gameface scales rem); text shadows stay in px (`UI_BUILD.style.remProperties`), so the HUD outline is one device pixel at every interface scale instead of a 1.25 or 1.5 px offset that smears into a soft halo. Crisp HUD: a label's left/top is snapped to whole device pixels (`shared/lib/pixel-snap`, the interface scale from `screenScale()`), so a fractional scale does not start every panel between pixels; `TabularText` takes a whole-px `digitWidth` (a 0.58em cell is 8.7px at 15px and puts every digit between pixels); client images draw at their rendition size with `ClientIcon native` (`shared/lib/client-icon` `renditionBox`: the ally and enemy class icons are 17×21, the white ones 16×16, measured in the RU 1.45 gui packages), never squeezed into another box. Inline SVG: the wrapper is sized in rem and the `<svg>` fills it (`100%`, a unitless `viewBox` and stroke widths), and the `<svg>` carries the `svg-canvas` mixin (`_gameface.scss`), which resets the text lengths it would inherit (font size, line height, letter spacing) to unitless values: Gameface logs `Rem units are not supported in SVGs` for every SVG element that inherits a rem length, thousands of lines a battle as the reticle arcs and timers redraw. A panel resized with the wheel is still a `transform: scale()` (rem is the root size, so a subtree cannot be scaled through it), and at 100 % it draws without one; Gameface parses colours with alpha only as `rgba(r, g, b, a)` and silently drops `rgb(r g b / a)` (text shadows and borders vanished that way): `postcss-color-functional-notation` rewrites every colour in the build, since esbuild lowers it only in plain colour properties (CSS is minified by esbuild, not Lightning CSS, which would fold `flex-direction` + `flex-wrap` into `flex-flow` and `1 1 auto` into `flex: auto`, both missing in Gameface), and the HUD tokens (`_hud.scss`) are written as `rgba()`; the bundle test fails on any space-separated colour left. The window's own surfaces are the `win-*` tokens of `packages/design-tokens/scss/_window.scss` (layered gradients for the frame, bars, cards and controls, an inner top highlight, soft shadows, the accent fill and its glow ring, the preview stage): `_gameface.scss` wraps them in the mixins `raised`, `well`, `control`, `accent-fill` and `ease(...)` (CSS transitions on `duration-base`), so a component names a surface, never a colour. Every card that changes something on screen shows it: a HUD panel draws its own widget on its page, `CARD_PREVIEWS` in `widgets/component/component-card/config` maps a plain card to a drawn preview (`hangar_tweaks` → the carousel with the chosen number of rows, `CarouselPreview`), and every list row is the same grid (icon, title with tags over a two-line hint, a 104×58 thumbnail box, the arrow, the switch; a row without a switch of its own says so in the switch column, and a row without a picture keeps the box's place): the thumbnail is the panel's widget scaled to fit the box (`FitBox`, measured again for a few frames because Gameface lays a new element out late, the component's icon when the widget would shrink below a readable 30 %) or the `thumb` picture the feature sends; fields marked `advanced` form the page's «Дополнительно» group. The «///» mark (title bar, hangar button) is `LOGO_SHAPES` from `@otmetki/icons/shapes`, drawn from the icon sprite; the ModsList `icon.png` is the same mark on the tile colours, rasterised at build time with resvg.
- **Code layout (`ui-web/src`):** a standard Vite React app with the web client's Feature-Sliced Design layers under `src/` ([docs/architecture/fsd.md](../../../docs/architecture/fsd.md)) and no `app` layer; `@/` is `ui-web/src/` in tsconfig, Vite and Vitest. The page entries are files at the `src/` root named after their page, each one thin: `main.tsx` (the settings window, `index.html`), `hud.tsx` (`hud.html`), `viewer.tsx` (`viewer.html`, the hit viewer), `advisor.ts` (the preset advisor script, `--mode advisor`) and `dev.ts` (the browser mock bridge `ui:dev` injects); the page entries import `@/shared/lib/engine-shims/install` first, then call `onDomReady(() => mountOnce({ id, node }))` with the view and its root id, then the page's global stylesheet (`shared/styles/{settings,hud,viewer}.scss`). `views/` the whole screens with their page config: `settings` (the window: `App` with its `use-app` hook subscribes to the bridge and picks the section), `hud` (the HUD page: anchors, docked columns, labels), `hit-viewer`, `preset-advisor` (the React-free script). `widgets/<domain>/<slice>` the window's blocks: `window/{window-frame,header,sidebar,notice}`, `component/{component-card,component-list}` (section and search pages; the view passes `component-card` in), `account/account-card`, `replay/replays-browser`, `hud/hud-editor`. `features/<domain>/<slice>` what the player does: `component/{toggle-component,edit-setting,search-components}`, `window/{window-menu,undo-change}`, `account/bind-account`, `profile/manage-profiles`, `replay/manage-replay`, `hud/edit-panels` (drag and wheel-resize a panel in HUD edit mode) and `hud/widget-registry` (widget kind → entity, `HudSample`; it composes every HUD entity, so it sits a layer above them). `entities/<domain>/<slice>`: `window/window-state` (the nanostores store: state, view, search, undo; `changeSetting`/`toggleSwitch`/`resetComponent`/`undoLast`, the component filters and search in `lib/components`, `useT`), `replay/replay`, `hud/<kind>` (one per HUD widget kind) and `hud/panel-layout` (panel geometry, hit testing, the screen read). `shared/` is project-agnostic: `api/gameface` (the one typed adapter over the Gameface globals `model`, `engine`, `viewEnv`, `subViews`, plus `api/gameface/mock` and the dev harness `api/gameface/dev-bridge`), `api/protocol` (zod/mini schemas, inferred types, `parseState`, `send`), `api/hud-protocol`, `config`, `i18n`, `lib` (one folder per concern, hooks as `use-<x>`: `dom` (`onDomReady`, `mountOnce`), `wheel-scroll`, `format-number`, `design-screen`, …), `styles`. `ui-kit/` the design system, imported only as `@/ui-kit`: `atoms` (button, icon, icon button, input with a drawn placeholder, toggle, badge, logo), `molecules` (segmented, page header, confirm, action bar, detail list, empty, fit box), `organisms` (the div-based list, scroll area) and `hud` (the HUD page's plates, glyphs, numbers, bars, radial timer, silhouette). Imports go downward only (entries → `views → widgets → features → entities → shared`, `ui-kit` beside `shared`), never between slices of one layer, and through a slice's `index.ts`: `@/<layer>/…` across slices, short relative paths inside one (`shared/lib/icon-sprite` reaches `shared/config` relatively because the Vite config loads it without the alias). Components only render; state, handlers and derived values come from each slice's `model/hooks/use-*`, constants from its `config/`.
- **React on Gameface:** the pages run React 19 (`react-dom/client` `createRoot`; `shared/lib/dom` `mountOnce` renders the first frame inside `flushSync`) with nanostores through `@nanostores/react` and generic hooks from `@siberiacancode/reactuse` (`useWindowEvent`, `useInterval`). Gameface lacks host APIs React expects, so `shared/lib/engine-shims` is the first import of both pages: it adds `queueMicrotask`, a microtask `setImmediate` when there is no `MessageChannel` (React's scheduler picks one of them when its module loads, so the shims must run before any React import; `otmetki/modpack-engine-shims-first` in the root `eslint.config.mjs` keeps that import order), and hides a `null` `window.event`, which React reads as the current event. - **Gameface CSS** (`ui-web/stylelint.config.mjs`, run by the repo's `bun run lint:css`). ## Hangar and client-settings components

Each is a feature package with a config.json switch (on by default) and a components.json section. The four client-settings components write **only standard client settings the game's own settings window offers**, through the settings core (`core/client/native`), only in the hangar, and only when the player changes a value in the window, loads a profile (`component_settings`) or presses «Рекомендуемые настройки» on a card: the value `native` («Как в игре») never touches the game's setting, and a later change in the game's own settings window is never overridden. A new section starts at `native` on every install, so a fresh install writes nothing by itself (`core/client/native.ClientDefaults`), with one exception the product owner decided: the minimap's last-seen spots and names (below); the recommended button keeps the values it replaces in `state.json` (`native_backup`) and the card then offers «Вернуть как было».

### replay_manager — replay manager

- **What:** a replays screen in the settings window (the component's page, `kind: "replays"`, drawn by `ui-web/src/widgets/replay/replays-browser`): the player's own replays in the client's replay folder (the header's `playerID` must be the logged-in account), up to the 1000 newest files. Each row: the map's small picture and the hangar vehicle icon (client images `gui/maps/icons/map/small|stats/<mapName>.png`, `vehicle/<playerVehicle>.png`, checked with `ResMgr.isFile`), tier and localized short name (`items.vehicles`), map, battle type (`ARENA_BONUS_TYPE` grouped: random, ranked, onslaught, frontline, ...), date, a result stripe, damage, assist (radio + tracks, as the results screen), frags, the mastery badge, the site state and a star. The details pane adds the map picture with the result, survival, spotted, XP, credits, shots / hits / penetrations, damage received and blocked, battle length and time alive — all from the recorder's own `personal` entry and the `common` block of the replay's results; the `vehicles`, `players` and `avatars` blocks are never read (fair play). A replay left before the end has only its arena block and says so.
- **Search, filters, sort:** instant, in the page: text (map, tank, file name), result, map, vehicle, tier, battle type, period (day, week, month), favourites; sort by date, damage, assist, XP, frags, battle length or size, both ways; a summary of the selection (shown of total, win rate, average damage, assist and XP). The list is virtualised (a 68 px row, drawn rows only) and follows the UI scale.
- **Watch:** the client plays a replay only from a fresh start (`BattleReplay.autoStartBattleReplay`, RU 1.45), so «Смотреть» (after a confirmation) writes `mods/configs/otmetki/replay_manager_play.json` and restarts the client (`BigWorld.savePreferences()` + `restartGame()`, as the client's own settings window does). On the next start the entry script's `boot()` reads and deletes the request, and — when it is under 3 min old and the file still exists — puts the client's `BattleReplayMachine` in place of the gameplay machine and answers `getAutoStartFileName()` with the file: the path a replay opened from Windows takes. When the replay stops (Esc or «Закончить») the client restarts instead of quitting. Only replays of the exact running version (`clientVersionFromExe` = `BigWorld.getProductVersion()`) can be started; others show why. Refused in battle and while the client records or plays a replay.
- **Upload:** «Загрузить на сайт» asks `replay_upload` through `core.events.EVENT_REPLAY_UPLOAD_REQUEST` (its opt-in `upload_replays` switch and the binding apply; the answer is shown as a notice); the page shows why the button is off (switch off, not bound, component missing, no battle id). Uploaded replays link to `/replays/<id>` («На сайте», «Разбор готов»), queued ones say «В очереди».
- **Files:** rename (Windows-safe names, same extension), delete (with a confirmation), favourites (by battle id, else file name; kept with the site ids in `replay_manager_<account>.json`), open the folder, refresh. Headers are read once per file version and kept in `replay_manager_headers.json`; new or changed files are read in 150 ms slices of the hangar tick while the window shows the page, with a progress bar.
- **Auto names (opt-in):** with `auto_rename` on, the replay of each own battle is renamed after the battle from `name_template` (macros `{date}`, `{time}`, `{map}`, `{vehicle}`, `{tier}`, `{result}`, `{damage}`, `{xp}`, `{frags}`, `{arena}`; Windows-safe, same extension). The file is matched by its header (the battle id, else the start time within 5 min), never by its name; checked every 15 s in the hangar once the file has not changed for 10 s; a battle without a replay is dropped after 30 min; an existing target name is never overwritten. The replay upload matches by header too, so a renamed file still uploads.
- **Analysis notice:** for the replays this game session uploaded (`replay_uploaded`), the hangar asks `POST /mod/me/replays` every 60 s for up to 6 h (at most 20 ids, signed like `/mod/ingest`); when the site reports `parsed`, a notification «разбор реплея готов на сайте (точность 83%, урон 2 150, пробитий 7)» and the row's badge «Разбор готов». A server without the endpoint answers 404, which stops the polling for the session ([contract](contract/replay-analysis.schema.json)).
- **Switch:** `hangar_replay_manager`. **Section:** `notify_analysis`, `auto_rename` (off by default), `name_template`.
- **Left out:** the client's own battle-results window for a replay (it needs patches of the results service), bulk delete.

### hangar_tweaks — hangar

- **What:** the carousel options of the client (`carouselType` one/two rows, `doubleCarouselType` tile size), three to five carousel rows on top of them, and three free quick actions on the selected vehicle, with confirmations: demount every piece of equipment the client marks removable, send the crew to the barracks (refused when the barracks are known to be full), and bring the previous crew back (the client's own «Вернуть экипаж», refused without a remembered crew). All refuse while the vehicle is in battle, in the queue or in a platoon, and run the client's own item processors (signatures checked against the RU 1.45 source: `TankmanUnload` takes the vehicle's inventory id). Demounting goes one slot at a time: each request is built from the vehicle as `IItemsCache.items.getVehicle(invID)` holds it after the previous answer, and stops at the first failure; each answer's own text is shown through `SystemMessages.pushMessagesFromResult`, as the hangar's buttons do. The barracks check is `IItemsCache.items.freeTankmenBerthsCount()`.
- **Style removal:** «Снять стиль» (confirmed) takes the style off the selected vehicle the way the customization window does (an empty outfit for every season through the client's `OutfitApplier`); the style goes back to the depot. Refused without a style or while the vehicle is locked.
- **Carousel rows:** `carousel_rows` (`native`, `1`..`5`; the `single` / `double` of older files read as `1` / `2`). `1` and `2` are the game's own `carouselType` (0 and 1); `3`..`5` write the two-row type (its tile size applies) and override `TankCarousel.as_rowCountS` (RU 1.45 `gui/Scaleform/daapi/view/lobby/hangar/carousels/basic/tank_carousel.py`, the class every mode carousel extends) so the carousel gets that row count in place of the game's 2; the Flash `TankCarousel.as_rowCount` lays out any row count (`HorizontalScrollerViewPort`), so no Flash patch is needed. A single row the player picks in the game's own carousel filter stays a single row; the carousels already shown are laid out again on a change (kept in a `weakref.WeakSet`), and the game's count comes back when the switch goes off. UNVERIFIED on Lesta 1.45: the event-entry viewport (`ExtendedHorizontalScrollerViewPort`, while an event icon shows at the carousel's right end) assumes pairs of rows, so 3 or 5 rows may shift there, and the mode carousels were not checked in the client. Like `interface_scale_exact` this bends the «client settings only through the game's own options» rule on purpose (the user's request, the competitor `advancedtankcarousel` did it with a Flash patch); the native choice is the default.
- **Interface scale:** `interface_scale` (`native`, `auto`, `x1`, `x1_25`, `x1_5`, `x1_75`, `x2`), the game's own option, written as the index of that scale in the screen's scale list; a scale the screen does not offer is left alone.
- **Exact interface scale:** `interface_scale_exact`, a percent 0..300 (0 = off, the default; under 50 counts as off): any scale between the game's steps, 130 % for one. Applied at run time only, in the hangar, through the setting's own `InterfaceScaleSetting.setSystemValue(scale)` (stage and glyph cache) and `InterfaceScaleManager.changeScale(scale)` (RU 1.45 `account_helpers/settings_core`); never written into the preferences, so the game's own scale returns when it is switched off (`scaleChanged()`) or the mod is removed. It is put back after anything else set a scale (`interfaceScale.onScaleChanged`: the game's option, a resolution change). UNVERIFIED on Lesta 1.45: how the battle page keeps a scale outside the stock list. This bends the «client settings only through the game's own options» rule on purpose (the user's request after the competitor study, `extended-interface-scaling`), kept off by default.
- **Switch:** `hangar_tweaks`. **Section:** `carousel_rows`, `carousel_tiles`, `interface_scale`, `interface_scale_exact`, `quick_actions`.
- **Left out:** hiding the hangar tutorial hints (no side-effect-free client API could be verified for Lesta 1.45) and an accelerated crew training switch: on 1.45 the crew widget turns it on by itself for elite and premium vehicles and no client button sends the `XP_TO_TMAN` vehicle flag, so hangar_info shows the state instead.

### minimap — minimap

- **What:** size (`AccountSettings` `minimapSize` 0..5, what the battle minimap and its +/- keys use; it is not a settings-core option), transparency (`minimapAlpha`, with its `minimapAlphaEnabled` switch turned on for any transparency above zero: the client counts the alpha only while it is on), the game's extended minimap features (`showVehModelsOnMap`, field `vehicle_names`: Alt / always, «Как в игре»; the recommended value is always, the game's default). That one option also keeps a vehicle that left sight at its last spotted point (`ArenaVehiclesPlugin._hideVehicle`, RU 1.45 `minimap/plugins.py`), so the component never writes `never`: only the game's own settings window offers it, and a `never` stored by earlier builds reads as «Как в игре»; the field is labelled «Последние места и названия техники», what the option shows (`as_showVehiclesNameS` and the last-seen point, `ArenaVehiclesPlugin.__showFeatures`), and while the client itself is off the recommended values (the game at `never`) the card offers «Рекомендуемые настройки» even when the section holds them (`ClientDefaults._client_holds`, `native_settings.client_holds`); and the player's own range circles (`minimapViewRange`, `minimapMaxViewRange`, `minimapDrawRange`).
- **The one-time switch (the exception to «write only on the player's change»):** with `showVehModelsOnMap` at `never` the minimap shows no vehicle names and no last-seen spots at all, which players kept reporting as a broken minimap; the owner decided the minimap must show both out of the box. So `vehicle_names` starts at `always` in a new section (`native_settings.native_choices` with the component's `once`, `features/minimap/model/constants.ONCE`), and a file older than companion revision 8 moves a `native` the player never set (no `minimap.vehicle_names` in config.json's `user_set`) to `always` (`RETIRED_VALUES`). On the first hangar (`ClientDefaults.apply_once`, `native_settings.once_step`), once the player's server settings have arrived (`settings_synced()`, the settings cache's `isSynced`; before that the settings core answers every read with the option's default, so the switch waits for `onSyncCompleted`): with the field at `always`, not chosen by the player, and the game at `never` (0), it writes `2` once through the settings core and logs «minimap: the game hid last-seen spots and names (never); switched to always once»; a game at Alt puts the field back to «Как в игре» instead; a game already at always changes nothing. `state.json` `native_once` records that it ran with its revision (`minimap@2`), so it never repeats: a `never` the player picks later in the game's own window, or «Как в игре» picked in ours, stays. The switch is at revision 2 (`ONCE['revision']`): revision 1 read the game's value before the server settings arrived, saw the default and could mark itself done without switching, so an install where revision 1 ran (`minimap` in `native_once`) runs it once more, putting a field the player never chose back to `always` first.
- **Switch:** `minimap_tweaks`. **Fair play:** vanilla options only; a test checks that no setting name concerns enemies, directions, tracers, destroyed objects or spotting. **Left out:** zoom beyond the client's own size range (Flash patch); lost-enemy markers of our own, gun directions, arty tracers (forbidden); the stock last-spotted points stay exactly as the client draws them.

### camera — camera

- **What:** camera presets (`preset`: `sniper` = enter the sight at x8 without the dynamic camera, `balanced` = at x4, `dynamic` = at x2 with the dynamic camera; all with horizontal stabilisation), the zoom on entering sniper mode (`sniperZoom`, the game's own option: remember the last one, x2, x4, x8; the client has no option for the list of zoom steps), the dynamic camera and horizontal stabilisation. Choosing a preset puts the fields it covers that the same change did not set back to «Как в игре», so the preset fills them; a field set by hand after it wins.
- **Switch:** `camera_tweaks`. **Left out:** camera distance and zoom beyond the client's own options, free-look / pitch limits, the commander camera and sway removal. All of them override the camera configuration (PMOD-style) rather than a setting the game exposes.

### crosshair — crosshair presets

- **What:** presets (`classic`, `minimal`, `contrast`, `clean`) over the client's own reticle settings (`arcade` / `sniper`: opacity and style index of the net, centre mark, gun mark, mixing, reload, condition, cassette and zoom indicator; the player's other parts are kept), for arcade, sniper or both; and the client's server-reticle switch (`useServerAim`).

### hit_viewer — hit viewer

- **Recording (battle):** `core.client.battle.on_shot_with_own_vehicle` hooks `Vehicle.showDamageFromShot` and passes only the shots between the player's own tank and one other vehicle (on the own tank by another one: `received`; on another vehicle by the own one: `dealt`); a shot between two other vehicles and every splash (`showDamageFromExplosion`: artillery, air strikes) never reach it. Each hit keeps the packed points as the client received them, the part and outcome of the last drawn point (`core.shot_points`), the shell code and calibre (the shooter's gun shot whose shell has the hit's `effectsIndex`, from `arena.vehicles[id]['vehicleType'].gun.shots`, as poliroid BattleHits' `getShellParams` finds it: the shot effects group itself carries no shell type and only a rough calibre), the hit vehicle's turret yaw and gun pitch (`Vehicle.getAimParams()`, as BattleHits' `processShot`), the other vehicle's short name and class, and the damage the own feedback reported (`DAMAGE` / `RECEIVED_DAMAGE`, matched by vehicle id within 1 s). The hit vehicle is kept as its type and its chassis, turret and gun compact descriptors only (never the packed descriptor, which carries equipment). Nothing is analysed in battle. File `hit_viewer_<account>.json` (the last `keep_battles`, 20; up to 160 hits a battle), written on the next hangar entry (`core.client.storage.held_for_hangar`), never in or at the end of a battle. It does not reuse `battle_hits_<account>.json`: that book belongs to `battle_results`, stores fractions of the part's box for its 2D schematic only, and its cleaner drops unknown keys; moving the «Попадания по мне» page onto this book is a follow-up.
- **Viewer (hangar):** poliroid's BattleHits (MIT, `refs/mods/poliroid__battle-hits`) is the model: a separate hangar view, the model taking the screen, a compact list at the side. The page (`otmetki/ui/hit_viewer`, `viewer.html` from `ui-web/src/viewer.tsx` (`views/hit-viewer`), shipped and registered by the ui package) is a Gameface **lobby sub view** loaded in place of the hangar view (`LoadGuiImplViewEvent(GuiImplViewLoadParams(layout, ViewerView, ScopeTemplates.LOBBY_SUB_SCOPE))`, `ViewFlags.LOBBY_SUB_VIEW`), as the stock Gameface views over the 3D hangar are (`maps_training_base_view`, `early_access_vehicle_view`): `app.setBackgroundAlpha(0)`, the lobby header menu hidden while it is open, and the page's drags and wheel sent as `move {dx, dy, dz}` to `CameraRelatedEvents.LOBBY_VIEW_MOUSE_MOVE` (the stock `_onMoveSpace`). Layout: title and close top left, the «По мне / По врагам» tabs with their counts top centre, the battle picker top right (each battle with the client's small map picture `gui/maps/icons/map/small/<geometry>.png`, the own team's result colour and word from the battle results that arrive after it (`battle_results` on the bus, only `common.winnerTeam` and the own team), the own tank with its tier, the date and both hit counts), the hit list (№, tank, result chip, part, shell, angle, armour, damage) at the left, as tall as its rows (heights in rem, the unit the client scales; inline px would not follow the interface scale), the selected hit's card (a header in the result's colour with the tank, result, part and damage, then shell and calibre, angle, nominal and effective armour) right under it; the list scrolls once the column is full. The page lays out in the part of the screen its view covers (`getViewGlobalPositionRem` / `getViewSizeRem`) above the lobby's bottom bar (45 design px, the Scaleform MessengerBar the sub view leaves on screen), on a design screen of at least 1920×1035 (`lib/viewer-frame`: never below the interface scale, up to twice it, `transform: scale` on the page's frame), and reports what it read once as `hit viewer: page screen …` in python.log; a row click or ←/→ and ↑/↓ step through the hits, Tab switches the tab. Before the first recorded battle the page opens with its empty state («Сыграйте бой — попадания по вашему танку появятся здесь» and a close button); a refused opening logs why (battle, queue, switch off, no Gameface page). Closing loads the stock hangar view back (`showHangar()`). The vehicle is swapped the way the stock vehicle preview does it, `g_currentPreviewVehicle.selectVehicle(intCD, strCD)` with the descriptor rebuilt from the recorded modules (`VehicleDescr(typeID).installComponent / installTurret`) and the stock style; closing calls `selectNoVehicle()` and `HangarCameraManager.resetCameraTarget(0)`. A point is placed in its part's collision box on the loaded model (`collisions.getBoundingBox(part)`, unpacked as `DamageFromShotDecoder.decodeSegment` does, `model/geometry`): the middle of the segment is the hit point and the segment its direction, as BattleHits' `HangarScene` places them. The client's own `decodeHitPoints` is not used: it also ray-tests the collision, which the hangar vehicle does not answer on the frame it reports itself loaded, so no hit decoded (python.log, 2026-10-05). Placing and measuring are retried every 0.1 s, 20 times, while the collision is missing or silent, then logged with the counts. The first armoured plate along each shot (`collisions.collideAllWorld`, the part's `materials`, then `commonConfig['materials']`) gives the angle from the normal, the nominal armour and the effective armour (nominal / cos of the angle left after the shell's normalisation: AP 5°, APCR 2°, widened by 1.4 × calibre / (2 × armour) on an over-match; HEAT and HE none — the client never loads `normalizationAngle`), saved back into the book. The selected hit is drawn in 3D the way BattleHits' `HangarScene` draws it: the turret and gun take the recorded pose (`compoundModel.node('turret' / 'Gun', matrix)`), BattleHits' own shell model (AP, APCR, HEAT, HE) and outcome marker (ricochet, not penetrated, penetration, critical) are `BigWorld.Model`s with a `Servo` in the hangar space, placed at the hit point and turned along the shot, their signal a fixed world matrix: the hit point goes from its part into the vehicle through the descriptor's part offsets and the recorded yaw and pitch (`model/placement`, as BattleHits' `Vehicle.__updateComponents`), then through the vehicle model's own matrix; a signal tied to a model node dangled after a vehicle swap (python.log 2026-10-06: «Usage of dangling PyModelNodeAdapter», the shell at the world origin and the camera aimed at the floor), and the camera flies to the hit looking along the shell's path (`moveCamera(point, yaw, -pitch, 5.5, 0.5, Math.Vector2(2.9, 9))`: the manager keeps the limits and `setMinDist` writes into them, so a tuple raised in every later vehicle load and left the hangar on «обновление ангара»). The models ship unmodified at their own path `res/content/battlehits/` (asset set `battlehits_models`, licence and notices with them). Nothing is drawn over the scene in 2D, as in BattleHits: a marker projected in Python on a 40 ms timer and pushed through the view model reached the page a frame or more after the camera moved and jumped while it orbited.
- **Zones, filter, totals, armour profile:** each row names the hit's zone instead of the bare part (`model/zones`: the impact point's middle as fractions of its part's box, +z front, +y up; hull: upper / lower plate (front band split at half height), side, rear; turret: front, side, rear; gun and running gear as they are; a rough split, not a given tank's plates, and the panel says so). Over the list, result chips («Все», then each result present with its count) filter the rows on the page only; ←/→ step through the filtered rows and a filter that hides the selected hit selects its first row. The tab's totals (`tab_summary`): the damage of its penetrations and, on «По мне», the share of hits the armour held (ricochet, block, no damage), on «По врагам» the share that penetrated. On «По мне» the «Броня танка» panel (right, under the battle picker, foldable) reads every kept battle on the same own tank (`model/profile.armor_profile`, by the own target's compact descriptor; only received hits on the own tank): battles, hits, the share the armour held, per zone the penetrations of the hits as a bar, and once there are 5 hits the weak zone (the most penetrated, at least 2 penetrations) with a short tip for it (`hv_advice_<zone>`). The screen caches the profile by the own tank and the kept battles' ids and hit counts, so the settle loop's pushes do not rebuild it. `bun run ui:dev` serves `/pages/viewer.html` with a mock bridge (`views/hit-viewer/lib/viewer-mock`, its state generated from this model: `lib/viewer-protocol/_tests/fixtures/viewer-state.sample.json`).
- **Opens from:** its own ModsList entry (`client/mods_list`, `addModification(id='otmetki_hit_viewer', lobby=True)`, the latest battle; `updateModification(enabled=False)` from the app's `enqueued` until `dequeued` or the next hangar, as poliroid's BattleHits greys its entry in the queue), the component card (`ui_actions`: «Открыть просмотр», «Очистить записи»), its page of recorded battles («Открыть» per row), and `hit_viewer_open(battle_id)` on the bus (`core.events.EVENT_HIT_VIEWER_OPEN`) from the «Посмотреть попадания» button of a battle results history row and of a replay manager row; those show the button only for the battles the viewer answers to `hit_viewer_battles(reply)` (`core.events.hit_viewer_battles(bus)`). Opening sends `settings_close` (the ui host closes the settings window). Esc and the close button close it; entering a battle closes it.
- **UNVERIFIED on Lesta 1.45:** the preview swap outside the preview view, `appearance.collisions` of the hangar vehicle (its boxes and `collideAllWorld`), the models riding on the part nodes, the camera's `source` / `target` / `pivotMaxDist` and the yaw/pitch convention of `moveCamera`, `setInputArea` passing the mouse to the hangar, a plain `WINDOW` on `TOP_WINDOW` sized by the page.
- **Switch:** `hangar_hit_viewer` (on). **Section `hit_viewer`:** `keep_battles` (1–30), `record_received`, `record_dealt`.

### Install by hand

Копируйте в `<игра>/mods/<версия клиента>/` **только один набор**: либо все split-пакеты из `dist/` (`net.triotmetki.*_<v>.mtmod` и `otmetki.companion_<v>.mtmod`), либо один `dist/single/otmetki.<v>.mtmod`, никогда оба. Плюс зависимости: `net.openwg.gameface_*.mtmod` (окно настроек и HUD) и `me.poliroid.modslistapi_*.mtmod` (вход в окно настроек). `otmetki.companion_<v>.mtmod` — это split-пакет компаньона, а не единый пакет. The single package is the union of the split ones under the companion's id, so both sets together mount every file twice; `core/client/packaging` logs a `WARNING: ... holds both the single package` line at start when it finds that (it cannot unload either set: delete one).

Package versions come from `packages/core/version.py`, `packages/companion/version.py` and each `features/<id>/__init__.py`; `--single` and the catalogue's `modpackVersion` take the release version, `version` in [package.json](package.json).

Each package is a stored (uncompressed) zip with explicit directory entries, `meta.xml` and `res/scripts/client/gui/mods/...`; the ui package also carries `res/gui/gameface/mods/triotmetki/ui/*` and `res/mods/configs/res_map/net.triotmetki.ui.json`, which are never compiled. The split packages never ship the same file, and `--single` is their union. The `<dependencies>` block in `meta.xml` is for installers and people. Whether the client reads it is **unverified**, and the code never relies on it (see [Load order](#load-order)).

**Release builds must ship `.pyc`.** The production client loads only `mod_*.pyc`; it reads `.py` only in development mode. A source-only package does not load in a live client. Pick the compiler with `--compiler auto|owg|py27` (see `tools/build/compilers.py`):

- **`owg`**, tried first: OpenWG [owg_python_compiler](https://gitlab.com/openwg/owg-python-compiler).
  - A C++ tool that writes CPython 2.7 bytecode without Python 2.7, reproducibly (fixed header timestamp, stable `co_filename`).
  - It has no binary releases: build the tag with CMake, as the modpack job of [.github/workflows/release.yml](../../../.github/workflows/release.yml) does. Pass `--owg-compiler PATH` or set `$OWG_PYTHON_COMPILER`.
  - **Unverified:** that `--filename-root scripts` gives `co_filename` `scripts/client/gui/mods/...`. Check a traceback in `python.log` after the first owg-built release.
- **`py27`**: a Python 2.7 interpreter running `py_compile`, with `dfile` set to the in-package path. Looked up as `--python27`, `$OTMETKI_PY27`, `$PYTHON27`, `py -2.7`, `python2.7`, `python2`, `C:\Python27\python.exe`.

Without either, the packages carry `.py` sources and the build prints a warning. They load in development clients only.

### Publishing a release

Releases live on our VPS only (no S3, CDN or GitHub Releases), under `https://triotmetki.ru/downloads/`. Bump `version` in [package.json](package.json) (the single source of the release version) with its `## <version>` CHANGELOG entry and the `VERSION`s of the packages that changed, update `otmetki.games` in the same file when the supported clients change, commit, push and run [.github/workflows/release.yml](../../../.github/workflows/release.yml); it has no inputs and releases only what has a version `releases.json` does not list yet (the modpack, the manager or both). It builds the release packages and the catalogue with the steps above and the manager installer in parallel, signs the release for the manager and publishes `modpack/<version>/` (the split packages, `otmetki.<version>.mtmod` from `dist/modpack/single/`, `catalog/`), `otmetki.mtmod` (the single package behind the /mod page's manual download), the installer and `releases.json`. The manager installs from that index ([manager README «Releases»](../manager/README.md#releases)); first-time setup is [docs/ops/deploy.md §4](../../../docs/ops/deploy.md#4-game-mod-releases-on-the-vps).

```bash
bun run dev:install marks_panel ui       # only these components (+ core, companion, the required ones, their deps)
bun run dev:install --dry-run            # print what would be built, downloaded, copied and removed; write nothing
bun run dev:watch [ID ...]               # install, then rebuild and reinstall the packages whose sources change
bun run dev:log                          # tail python.log, our lines only (--all: every line, --own: mods/configs/otmetki/otmetki.log)
bun run dev:uninstall [--dry-run]        # remove exactly what the dev loop installed
bun run dev:modpack                      # from the repo root: the same as dev:watch
```

- **The client.** `OTMETKI_GAME_DIR` names the client folder and wins. Otherwise the rules are the manager's ([detect](../manager/tauri/src/detect/mod.rs), ported to `tools/dev/client`): the clients Lesta Game Center lists (`%ProgramData%\Lesta\GameCenter\data\lgc_path.dat` -> its `preferences.xml`) plus the manager's `manualClients`; the pick is the manager's `selectedClient`, then the one LGC has selected, then the first usable Lesta 1.35+ client. The packages go into the folder `<Packages><Root>` of `paths.xml` names when it stays inside the client, else `mods/<version>/` from `version.xml`.
- **What gets installed.** The asked component ids (none: every package) with their dependencies by the manager's rules: the catalog's `required` entries, each package's own dependencies (core, companion) and its catalog `dependencies`. The packages are built like `build.py` without `--require-pyc`: bytecode with `owg_python_compiler` or a Python 2.7 when one is found (see [Build](#build)), `.py` sources otherwise, with a warning, because the production client loads only `mod_*.pyc` (set `OWG_PYTHON_COMPILER` or `OTMETKI_PY27`). OpenWG Gameface and ModsList join when a chosen component needs them (their catalog `requiredBy`): a copy already anywhere in `mods/<version>/` is used as it is, else the catalog's pinned `sourceUrl` is downloaded once into `dist/dev/thirdparty/` and checked against its size and sha256 on every use (`--offline` uses only that cache).
- **Where.** Everything goes into `mods/<version>/otmetki-dev/` (the client loads packages from subfolders: `paths.xml` has `mode="recursive"`), with `otmetki-dev.json` listing every file the dev loop wrote and its sha256. A reinstall copies only what changed and removes what it wrote before and no longer wants; `uninstall` removes exactly the listed files that still have their hash, leaves (and reports) one changed by hand, and deletes the folder only when nothing else is in it. The user's other mods and the rest of `mods/<version>/` are never touched.
- **Next to a manager install it refuses.** When the manager has installed the modpack in that client (its `%LOCALAPPDATA%\TriOtmetki\clients\<client key>\manifest.ini`, `OTMETKI_STATE_ROOT` overrides the root, or our packages lying in `mods/<version>/`), `install` and `watch` stop with that list: two copies of one package id break the client, and moving the manager's files aside would only make its background check put them back. Uninstall the modpack in the manager first. The manager lists `mods/<version>/` without descending, so it does not see the dev folder either: do not install through the manager while a dev install is there (`dev:status` shows both).
- **No hot reload.** The client mounts its packages at start and has no reload for Python mods or Gameface pages, so every reinstall ends with a reminder to restart the client. While it runs it keeps its packages open: the watch mode reports that and retries the sync until the client is closed. The first start after OpenWG Gameface is installed restarts the client once by itself (`Stored new res_map.json`).
- **Watch.** `packages/`, `features/` and `assets/` are watched; a change rebuilds the packages that ship the file or whose folder holds it (`packages/ui/gameface/` -> `ui`, so `bun run ui:build` reaches the client too), tests and caches excepted, debounced.
- **Another server.** The dev install's manifest is what lets `server_url` in `config.json` point at a local API (`http://localhost:4000`); a release install ignores it (see the config table).
- **Preview capture.** A dev install (the `otmetki-dev.json` manifest or `OTMETKI_DEV=1`; `companion/capture`, started by the app host only when `is_dev_install()`, so a release install has no such keys) takes real screenshots for the component catalogue: Ctrl+Shift+F12 calls the client's own `BigWorld.screenShot('png', 'screenshots/otmetki_<id>')` (the client appends `_NNN`, the same folder as its own `shot_NNN.jpg`) and logs the path, Ctrl+Shift+F11 picks the next component id from `mods/configs/otmetki/capture.json` (`python tools/build/previews/capture --prepare` writes it; without it, the installed features). `python tools/build/previews/capture` then crops, scales and packs the shots into `catalog/previews/<id>.png` ([catalog/previews/CAPTURE.md](catalog/previews/CAPTURE.md): what to shoot, with which setting).
- **Log level.** `core/log` has no level switch, so the dev install writes no verbose-logging flag; `dev:log` filters the `[OTMETKI]` lines and the tracebacks after them.

## Durable settings (`%APPDATA%\TriOtmetki`)

Mod installers and cleaners can delete `mods/configs`, so the files a player cannot recreate by hand have a second copy outside the game folder (`core/durable`, opened with `open_config(config_dir, name)`):

| File               | Written by                        | What                                                                    |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `credentials.json` | companion (binding), manager      | not mirrored: two different halves (below)                              |
| `config.json`      | companion (every settings change) | switches and data settings (table above)                                |
| `components.json`  | core HUD / every component        | one schema-checked section per component                                |
| `profiles.json`    | ui (profiles), manager            | `{version: 1, active, profiles: [...]}` (see [In-game UI](#in-game-ui)) |
| `state.json`       | companion (`app.save_state()`)    | the app's small state parts                                             |
| `saved_at.json`    | both sides, one per folder        | `{version: 1, files: {<name>: <unix seconds of the last save>}}`        |

- **Where:** `%APPDATA%\TriOtmetki\` (Windows: `os.environ['APPDATA']`; on Python 2 a value the ANSI code page cannot hold is re-read through `GetEnvironmentVariableW`). Without `APPDATA`: `~\AppData\Roaming\TriOtmetki` on Windows, `~/.config/TriOtmetki` elsewhere; without a home folder the mirror is off and plain `mods/configs/otmetki` files are used. Paths are text on both Pythons, so a Cyrillic user or game folder works.
- **Write-through:** every save writes `mods/configs/otmetki/<name>`, then the `%APPDATA%` copy, with the same stamp in each folder's `saved_at.json` and as the file's mtime. A failing `%APPDATA%` write never fails the save.
- **Restore on load:** each read compares the two copies. A copy's stamp is the later of its `saved_at.json` entry and its mtime, so a hand edit or the manager's write counts as a save. The game-folder copy is rewritten from `%APPDATA%` when it is missing, unreadable or older; the `%APPDATA%` copy is rewritten when it is missing or older; on a tie (within 10 ms) the game-folder copy wins. The newer copy always survives.
- **Credentials (`core.durable.open_secret_pair`, `companion/binding.CredentialStore`):** the game-folder `credentials.json` is `{accounts: {"<account id>": {device_id, account_id, bound_at}}}`, no secret; `%APPDATA%\TriOtmetki\credentials.json` is `{accounts: {"<account id>": {device_id, account_id, bound_at, secret_dpapi}}}` where `secret_dpapi` is base64 of `CryptProtectData(secret as UTF-8, entropy b"triotmetki-device-v1", CRYPTPROTECT_UI_FORBIDDEN)` in the CurrentUser scope (`core/durable/dpapi.py`, ctypes on crypt32; the manager uses the same format). A plaintext `secret` an older version wrote (in either copy) is read once and both copies are rewritten at once in the new format; plaintext is never written again, and a game-folder copy that is missing or carries other fields is rewritten from the `%APPDATA%` one. The halves are written separately, never mirrored, and get no `saved_at.json` entry. A blob DPAPI cannot open (another Windows user or PC, a roaming profile not synced yet) is no binding for now, but both its halves are kept untouched and it is tried again on the next start (logged once); without DPAPI or a durable folder a new binding lasts for the session only. The `%APPDATA%` copy is owner-only (`0600`) where the platform has mode bits. The device secret stays revocable on the site.
- **Not mirrored:** outboxes, replay queues, the marks history (per account, rebuilt or re-sent).
- **For the manager app:** it reads the same files from `%APPDATA%\TriOtmetki` (`credentials.json` in the split format above). When it writes one, it writes the whole JSON file atomically (temp file + rename) and sets the file's entry in that folder's `saved_at.json` to the current unix time; the mod picks the newer copy up at the next client start. The folder is shared by every client installation of the Windows user.
